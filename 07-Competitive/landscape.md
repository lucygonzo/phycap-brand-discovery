# Competitive Landscape

## Framework Overview

PhyCap operates at the intersection of physician-led investing and healthcare venture capital. The competitive landscape includes direct competitors, alternative models, and adjacent players who shape founder and LP expectations. This framework organizes competitors into six categories based on how they relate to PhyCap's positioning and market.

---

## Category 1: Head-to-Head Market Players
*Same problem (physician capital into healthcare startups), similar solution (physician-led VC fund)*

### Scrub Capital (Closest Parallel: Deep Dive Below)

**What they actually do:** Physician- and clinician-led VC fund writing $100K-$300K checks. Built a 1,000+ member clinician community (branded "Clinicians who VC" or CwVC, up from 650 at launch in late 2024) that functions as both a deal sourcing engine and diligence network. Fund 1 closed oversubscribed beyond its initial $10M target. Now led by 4 GPs: Dr. Rebecca Mitchell (Managing Partner, former clinical product leader at Teladoc and Livongo), Christina Farr (co-founder/GP, former CNBC health tech reporter with 350K+ social media followers), Dr. Jonathan Slotkin, and Kevin Foley. 13 known portfolio companies including unicorn Midi Health (menopause/women's health, $100M Series D, valued over $1B) and Canopy (healthcare worker safety, $22M Series B, protecting 300K+ workers across 60 health systems). Other portfolio companies include Spark Spine, Elion, RevBio, Oshi Health, Radial Health, Posterity Health, Conceivable Life Sciences, Lifeforce, and Clarity Pediatrics. Notably, GP Jonathan Slotkin co-founded RevBio, which is in the portfolio (a potential conflict of interest worth monitoring).

**Primary positioning:** Positions around clinician expertise broadly, emphasizing community scale and the idea that clinicians see problems before investors do. Extends reach through "The Differential" newsletter on Substack and the "Healthcare, Unscripted" unconference (March 2026, NYC, 100 attendees, co-hosted with Primary Ventures). Their VC Fellowship program (6-month, 10 hrs/week, $15/hr) creates a pipeline of clinician-investors. 50+ clinicians attend each pitch alongside 3 GPs.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Teal-dominant palette (primary #459b8f teal, secondary #003a40 dark teal, #487F84, #5F597E muted purple; accents #6ECA09 green, #FAB901 gold, #D0021B red). Fonts: Poppins for headlines, Hind for body. Clean design that leans startup-friendly rather than institutional. More community-forward than finance-forward in visual presentation.

**Built for:** Clinicians broadly defined, spanning 40+ medical specialties but also including nurses, PTs, OTs, and pharmacists (not physician-exclusive). Also targets healthcare founders who want clinical validation from a wide range of practitioners.

**Specific strength:** The 1,000+ member community is a genuine moat and growing fast (54% growth from launch to early 2026). That volume creates deal flow velocity, broad diligence capacity, and a built-in marketing engine. Christina Farr's 350K+ social media following and media background give Scrub Capital founder-facing visibility that most physician-led funds cannot match. Fund 1 closing oversubscribed validates LP demand. A portfolio with a unicorn (Midi Health) provides a strong signal to founders and future LPs.

**Specific weakness:** Breadth dilutes clinical authority. A pharmacist and a cardiac surgeon bring fundamentally different types of clinical insight. When Scrub says "clinician-led," the signal is weaker than "physician-led" or "specialist-led" because the credential range is so wide. Their check size ($100K-$300K) is also smaller than PhyCap's range, limiting their ability to lead rounds or secure meaningful ownership.

### Doctors Innovate Fund (Alumni Ventures Platform)

**What they actually do:** A physician-focused fund operating on the Alumni Ventures platform, which manages a 625K+ network of alumni-based investors. Provides physicians access to VC-style returns through a platform model with professional fund management.

**Primary positioning:** Positions as the way for doctors to invest in innovation alongside other physician peers, leveraging Alumni Ventures' institutional infrastructure.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Blue, institutional, clean. Inherits Alumni Ventures' polished but corporate aesthetic. Feels more like a financial product than a community or mission.

**Built for:** Physicians who want passive VC exposure without active involvement in diligence or portfolio support. Optimized for the physician who wants to write a check, not roll up sleeves.

**Specific strength:** The Alumni Ventures platform provides immediate infrastructure: fund administration, legal, compliance, investor relations, and a massive existing network (625K+). That is operational leverage no standalone Fund I can match.

**Specific weakness:** Platform model means physicians are investors, not operators. There is no mechanism for deep clinical diligence or GP-level engagement with portfolio companies. The fund is one of many on the Alumni Ventures shelf, which dilutes the physician-specific brand signal.

## Category 2: Alternative Solution Providers
*Same problem (getting clinical insight into healthcare investing), different solution (health system LP model)*

### LRVHealth

**What they actually do:** Boston-based healthcare VC with 25 years of operating history, 68 portfolio companies, and 3 unicorns. Focuses on Series A and B investments (later stage than PhyCap). Health system and payer LPs. Runs an "Inside Healthcare" platform that connects portfolio companies with health system partners for commercial traction. Made 9 investments in 2025. Notable portfolio includes Phreesia, IntelyCare, Change Healthcare (acquired), Olive (acquired), and Cleerly.

**Primary positioning:** Positions around being "inside healthcare" through institutional relationships rather than individual clinician expertise. 25-year track record and 3 unicorns provide significant institutional credibility.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Professional, enterprise-grade. Visual identity signals institutional credibility over community warmth. Designed to appeal to health system executives and payer leadership.

**Built for:** Healthcare founders at Series A and B who need health system distribution, and health systems/payers who want structured innovation pipelines.

**Specific strength:** Health system LP relationships create genuine commercial pathways for portfolio companies. This is a tangible value-add that individual physician investors cannot replicate. 25 years of track record, 68 companies, and 3 unicorns provide a level of institutional proof that no emerging manager can match. Active deployment pace (9 investments in 2025) signals continued momentum.

**Specific weakness:** Series A and B focus means limited overlap with PhyCap's seed-stage entry point; they are a potential co-investor or follow-on partner rather than a direct competitor for the same deals. Institutional LP relationships can slow decision-making and create conflicts of interest. Health system priorities (cost reduction, regulatory compliance) may not align with what founders need at seed stage. No individual clinical voice or specialty-level insight.

## Category 3: Industry Peers
*Same industry (healthcare VC), not direct competitors (different stage, thesis, or market)*

### Catalyst Health Ventures

**What they actually do:** MedTech-focused VC investing in transformative medical technologies. Targets medical devices and technologies that change clinical practice.

**Primary positioning:** Positions around transformative potential in medical technology, emphasizing the ability to identify technologies that will reshape care delivery.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Technology-forward, clinical. More device/hardware-oriented visual language than digital health.

**Built for:** MedTech founders building physical devices and hardware-dependent medical technologies.

**Specific strength:** Clear thesis focus on MedTech gives them pattern recognition and a network specifically tuned to FDA device pathways, manufacturing, and clinical trials for hardware.

**Specific weakness:** MedTech focus means they are not competitive in SaMD (Software as a Medical Device), digital health, or workflow optimization, which is where PhyCap's thesis concentrates.

### SV Health Investors

**What they actually do:** Global, multi-stage healthcare investor with $4B+ in total commitments across venture, growth, and buyout strategies.

**Primary positioning:** Positions as a scaled, global healthcare investment platform with decades of track record.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Institutional, global, established. Visual identity communicates scale and longevity rather than clinical intimacy or community.

**Built for:** Later-stage healthcare companies and institutional LPs seeking diversified healthcare exposure.

**Specific strength:** $4B in commitments and multi-stage capability means they can support companies from early venture through growth and buyout. Scale is their moat.

**Specific weakness:** Scale makes them irrelevant as a seed/Series A competitor. They do not compete for the same deals, but they set LP expectations for what "healthcare VC" looks like at maturity.

## Category 4: Attention Competitors
*Competing for the same audience's attention (physician investors, healthcare founders)*

### Rock Health

**What they actually do:** Three distinct entities: Rock Health Capital (early-stage venture fund, ~$50M AUM, $100K-$5M checks with ~$1.5M sweet spot, 4 portfolio companies), an enterprise advisory practice, and a nonprofit research organization. Overall track record spans 90 companies, 3 unicorns, 1 IPO, and 21 acquisitions. Published the definitive 2025 digital health funding report ($14.2B across 482 deals). Founder Halle Tecco sits on Scrub Capital's editorial board, creating a connection between these two competitors.

**Primary positioning:** Positions as the knowledge layer of digital health, combining investment with research that shapes industry narrative.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Clean, data-forward, editorial. Feels more like a media/research brand than a traditional fund. Strong content identity.

**Built for:** Digital health founders, industry analysts, corporate innovation teams, and journalists covering healthcare technology.

**Specific strength:** Content and research output is unmatched in healthcare VC. Rock Health reports are cited in board decks, fundraising materials, and media coverage across the industry. They own the "thought leadership" territory in digital health. The three-entity structure (fund, advisory, nonprofit) creates multiple revenue streams and touchpoints with the ecosystem. Rock Health Capital's check range ($100K-$5M) overlaps with PhyCap's at the lower end.

**Specific weakness:** Rock Health Capital is relatively small (~$50M AUM, 4 portfolio companies) compared to the research brand's visibility. Research brand has partially eclipsed the investment brand. Founders may engage with Rock Health content without considering them as investors. The fund itself is not physician-led and does not claim clinical depth. Halle Tecco's presence on Scrub Capital's editorial board suggests alignment between Rock Health and Scrub Capital, which could strengthen both against PhyCap.

## Category 5: Structural Parallels
*Different industry or niche, similar business model (specialist-led micro-fund)*

### Healthy Ventures

**What they actually do:** Women-led healthcare VC focused on healthcare infrastructure. Invests in companies building foundational systems for healthcare delivery.

**Primary positioning:** Positions around the intersection of women's leadership in VC and healthcare infrastructure investment.

**Visual identity and brand feel:** Warm, modern, mission-aligned. Visual identity communicates values and identity alongside investment capability.

**Built for:** Healthcare infrastructure founders and LPs who prioritize diversity in fund management.

**Specific strength:** Clear identity differentiation through women-led positioning creates a distinct brand in a space dominated by traditional fund structures. Aligns with broader LP demand for diverse fund managers.

**Specific weakness:** "Healthcare infrastructure" is broad enough to lack thesis specificity. The women-led positioning is a brand differentiator but not a clinical or technical moat.

## Category 6: Messaging and Positioning Benchmarks
*Similar challenges (establishing credibility as an emerging fund), excellent execution*

### Rock Health (also in Category 4)

Rock Health serves double duty as both an attention competitor and a messaging benchmark. Their ability to build a research-driven brand that generates inbound deal flow and LP interest is the playbook PhyCap should study. The key lesson: content that provides genuine analytical value (not thought leadership for vanity) creates durable brand equity.

### Scrub Capital (also in Category 1)

Scrub Capital's community-building execution is a messaging benchmark regardless of competitive dynamics. Growing from 650 members at launch to 1,000+ in roughly a year demonstrates that healthcare professionals will engage with an investing community if the onramp is clear and the value proposition is immediate. Their VC Fellowship, Substack newsletter ("The Differential"), and unconference ("Healthcare, Unscripted") form a multi-channel engagement model worth studying.
