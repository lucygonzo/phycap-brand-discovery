# Competitive Positioning Matrix

## Axis Selection and Rationale

### X-Axis: Community Focus (Institutional LP Model to Physician LP Model)

**Why this axis:** The fundamental structural question in physician-adjacent healthcare VC is who provides the capital. Funds range from traditional institutional LP bases (endowments, fund-of-funds, health systems) to models where individual physicians are the primary LP base. This axis captures the difference between funds that use physician credibility as a brand signal versus funds where physicians have genuine economic ownership.

- **Left (Institutional LP):** Capital comes from institutional sources. Physicians may advise or serve on advisory boards, but they are not the primary investors.
- **Right (Physician LP):** Capital comes primarily from individual physicians who invest as LPs. Physicians have direct economic stake and governance voice.

### Y-Axis: Clinical Depth (Generalist Clinician to Specialist Physician-Led)

**Why this axis:** The competitive landscape reveals a clear spectrum from funds that claim "clinical insight" broadly to funds where named physicians with specific specialties lead investment decisions. This axis captures the difference between a 650-member community of mixed clinicians and a 10-GP team with 13+ named specialties and 168+ years of practice.

- **Bottom (Generalist Clinician):** Clinical credibility comes from volume and breadth. Community includes non-physician clinicians. Clinical insight is crowd-sourced rather than individually attributed.
- **Top (Specialist Physician-Led):** Clinical credibility comes from named physicians with specific specialty expertise. Diligence is led by physicians whose clinical practice directly maps to the investment thesis.

## Competitor Placement Rationale

### PhyCap: Upper Right Quadrant
- **Clinical Depth:** Highest on the vertical axis. 10 GPs with 168+ years of combined clinical experience across 13+ named specialties. Every GP is a physician (not a mixed clinician base). Thesis areas (SaMD, Clinical Workflow, Women's Health) require specialty-specific diligence.
- **Community Focus:** Far right. Physician LP model where the capital base and the clinical expertise base overlap. Physicians are both investors and operators of the fund.

### Scrub Capital: Center Right, Below Center
- **Clinical Depth:** Below center. 650-member community includes nurses, pharmacists, and therapists alongside physicians. Clinical insight is crowd-sourced from a broad clinician base, not attributed to named specialists.
- **Community Focus:** Right of center. Clinician LP model, but the "clinician" definition is broader than physician-only. Still fundamentally a community-led fund.

### Doctors Innovate Fund: Lower Right
- **Clinical Depth:** Low. Physicians invest passively through the Alumni Ventures platform. No named physician GPs leading diligence. Clinical expertise is present in the LP base but not operationalized in investment decisions.
- **Community Focus:** Right. Physician LPs provide capital, but through a platform intermediary. The community is large (625K+ network) but engagement is transactional.

### LRVHealth: Center Left, Below Center
- **Clinical Depth:** Below center. Clinical insight comes through institutional relationships (health system and payer LPs) rather than individual physician expertise. The "Inside Healthcare" platform is structurally clinical but not physician-led.
- **Community Focus:** Left. Institutional LP base (health systems, payers). No physician LP community.

### SV Health Investors: Upper Left
- **Clinical Depth:** Above center. $4B in healthcare-specific commitments means deep healthcare expertise at the fund level, though not physician-led in the same way as PhyCap.
- **Community Focus:** Far left. Fully institutional LP model at scale.

### Rock Health: Lower Left
- **Clinical Depth:** Below center. Research-driven approach provides data-level clinical insight, but the fund is not physician-led and does not claim specialty clinical expertise.
- **Community Focus:** Left of center. Institutional and corporate LP base. No physician LP community.

### Catalyst Health Ventures: Upper Left
- **Clinical Depth:** Above center. MedTech specialization requires deep clinical understanding of device pathways, surgical workflows, and FDA processes. Specific but not physician-led.
- **Community Focus:** Left. Institutional LP model.

### Healthy Ventures: Center, Below Center
- **Clinical Depth:** Below center. Healthcare infrastructure focus is broad. Women-led positioning is a brand differentiator but not a clinical depth signal.
- **Community Focus:** Center. LP composition is not publicly detailed as either strongly institutional or physician-led.

## Strategic Implications

### The Upper Right Quadrant is Empty Except for PhyCap
No other fund combines specialist physician-led clinical depth with a physician LP community model. This is the Insider Precision territory. The quadrant is defensible because occupying it requires both assets simultaneously: you need the physician GPs (hard to recruit) and the physician LPs (hard to convert).

### Scrub Capital is the Nearest Competitor but on a Different Trajectory
Scrub Capital is moving horizontally (expanding community breadth) while PhyCap should move vertically (deepening specialist credibility). These are diverging strategies, which is favorable for PhyCap.

### The Lower Right Quadrant is Crowded
Passive physician investing (Doctors Innovate Fund model) and broad clinician communities (Scrub Capital model) both occupy the lower right. This is the easiest territory to enter and therefore the least defensible.

### Institutional Depth (Left Side) is Saturated
LRVHealth, SV Health Investors, Rock Health, and Catalyst Health Ventures all compete on the institutional side. PhyCap has no advantage and no reason to move in this direction.

## Gaps in This Section

1. **Axis placement is based on available public information, not proprietary data.** LP composition for several competitors (Healthy Ventures, Catalyst Health Ventures) is inferred rather than confirmed.
2. **The matrix is static.** Competitor positioning shifts over time. Scrub Capital may deepen its physician focus. Doctors Innovate Fund may activate its physician LPs more aggressively. A quarterly re-evaluation cadence would keep this useful.
3. **No third axis for thesis specificity.** The matrix captures clinical depth and community structure but not thesis focus. PhyCap's narrow thesis (SaMD, Workflow, Women's Health) is a differentiator that this two-axis model does not fully represent.
4. **Fund size is not captured.** The matrix does not account for the fact that SV Health ($4B) and PhyCap ($10M target) operate at fundamentally different scales. A founder choosing between them is making a very different calculus than this matrix suggests.
5. **No founder-facing validation.** The matrix reflects how funds position themselves, not how founders perceive them. Founder survey data would validate whether the axes chosen here match the dimensions founders actually evaluate.
