# Team Highlights

## GP Roster: Strategic Implications by Member

### Paul Slosar, MD, MHCDS
**Role**: Lead GP, Co-Founder
**Clinical Specialty**: Orthopedics / Spine Surgery
**Career Arc**: Practicing spine surgeon for 28 years in the San Francisco Bay Area. Served as Administrative Partner and President of his orthopedic group (2007-2020), managing the operational and strategic direction of a multi-physician practice. Served 11 years as Chief Medical Officer of Titan Spine, LLC, a medical device company focused on spinal implants, where he was instrumental in positioning the company for its acquisition by Medtronic in 2019. This CMO role gave Paul direct experience with the product development, regulatory, and commercialization cycle from the clinical side of the table. He also holds a certificate from Harvard Business School's Intensive Seminar in Value Based Health Care. He then pursued the Dartmouth Master of Health Care Delivery Science, which added systems-level healthcare architecture to his clinical and industry expertise. Paul co-founded PhyCap Fund and is a General Partner at FortySix Venture Capital.

**Strategic Implication for Diligence**: Paul evaluates portfolio companies through three lenses simultaneously: (1) as a surgeon who has used devices and workflows in the operating room for nearly three decades, (2) as a former CMO who navigated the medical device commercialization process through a successful acquisition by Medtronic, and (3) as an MHCDS graduate trained in systems thinking and delivery optimization. When reviewing a workflow optimization startup, Paul can assess whether the product will survive contact with actual clinical practice, not just whether the TAM slide is attractive. His Titan Spine experience (11 years as CMO, culminating in the Medtronic acquisition) means he understands FDA pathways, hospital procurement, and surgeon adoption patterns from the inside. His 13 years leading an orthopedic group as Administrative Partner and President add operational management perspective that most physician-investors lack.

**MHCDS Deep Dive**: The Dartmouth MHCDS is a 12-month interdisciplinary program jointly offered by the Tuck School of Business and the Geisel School of Medicine. The curriculum covers healthcare informatics, systems thinking, data-driven decision making, human-centered innovation, leadership and change management, and healthcare delivery optimization. Each cohort is approximately 45 accomplished healthcare professionals. The program is built on the intellectual foundation of Dr. John E. Wennberg's research into geographic variations in medical practice (the Dartmouth Atlas of Health Care), which revealed that how and where care is delivered matters as much as what care is delivered. Graduates come away with the ability to evaluate healthcare technologies not just as products but as interventions within complex delivery systems. This is the skill set that makes PhyCap's investment thesis (workflow optimization, SaMD, women's health) directly executable at the diligence level. Paul does not evaluate a SaMD product by asking "Does it work?" He evaluates it by asking "How does this product interact with the delivery system it will operate within? Where will it create friction? Where will it reduce waste? What workflow does it replace, and what happens to the adjacent workflows when it does?"

**Network**: Bay Area connections from 28 years of spine surgery practice and Titan Spine tenure. MHCDS alumni network (~45 graduates per cohort, program has run since ~2010, estimated 500+ alumni in senior healthcare leadership roles). Orthopedic surgery professional community. Harvard Business School alumni network. FortySix Venture Capital partnership network in Tulsa.

## The GP Roster as a Complete Diligence Team

The strategic significance of PhyCap's GP composition is not just that each individual is qualified. It is that together, they cover the full arc of human medicine:

| Life Stage / Clinical Domain | GP | Specialty |
|------------------------------|-----|-----------|
| Neonatal / Pediatric | Tim Martens | Pediatric Cardiothoracic Surgery |
| Reproductive / Women's Health | Robin Noble | OB-GYN |
| Acute / Emergency | Vipul Kella | Emergency Medicine |
| Musculoskeletal (Spine) | Paul Slosar | Orthopedics / Spine Surgery |
| Musculoskeletal (Upper Extremity) | Fred Liss | Orthopedics / Hand Surgery |
| Chronic / Primary Care | Joseph Jasser | Internal Medicine |
| Diagnostic / Imaging | Giovanni Lorenz | Radiology / Cardiothoracic |
| Frontline Workflow | Alex Tang | Physician Assistant |
| Systems Architecture | Paul Slosar + Robin Noble | MHCDS (Healthcare Delivery Science) |
| Research | Tim Martens + Jerald Dawkins | PhD |
| Business / Operations | Vipul Kella + Joseph Jasser + Alex Tang | MBA |

When a portfolio company enters PhyCap's diligence process, the product is evaluated from every clinical angle: Will it work in an emergency department? Does it fit OB-GYN workflows? How does it appear on diagnostic imaging? Does it survive the practical realities of a physician assistant's daily workflow? Will an internist refer patients to it? Can a spine surgeon integrate it into surgical planning?

No competing physician-led fund has this breadth. Scrub Capital has a broader clinician base (nurses, pharmacists, therapists) but fewer specialized physician GPs. Doctors Innovate Fund operates as a thematic fund within the Alumni Ventures platform and does not maintain a standing clinical diligence team. LRVHealth uses health systems and payers as strategic LPs rather than individual physician GPs.

This is the operational meaning of "Investing with Clinical Precision." The tagline is not marketing language. It is a description of how PhyCap actually evaluates deals.
