# Founding Story

## Why This Fund

The idea for PhyCap began with a recognition that Dutch Rojas and Paul Slosar shared: physicians sit at the center of healthcare delivery but have almost no structural role in deciding which technologies enter their practice. Venture capital firms invest billions in healthcare technology every year, but the clinicians who will ultimately use, adopt, or reject those technologies are rarely consulted before the investment is made. The result is a healthcare innovation ecosystem that optimizes for market opportunity rather than clinical utility.

Dutch Rojas, who is credited with the original concept (per RyOrtho, November 2024: "New Fund Gives Voice to Innovative Physicians"), saw the structural gap from the operational side: healthcare startups struggle to get genuine clinical feedback during their early stages because physicians are not part of the capital stack. Paul Slosar saw it from the clinical side: as a spine surgeon for 28 years, former CMO of Titan Spine, LLC for 11 years (a medical device company acquired by Medtronic in 2019), and Dartmouth MHCDS graduate, he had lived the full cycle of clinical innovation and understood both why it mattered and why it so often failed. His Titan Spine CMO role gave him direct experience seeing how venture capital evaluates medical technology from the industry side, completing a full-circle perspective: surgeon who uses devices, then CMO who commercializes devices, then MHCDS graduate who understands delivery systems, then GP who invests in the next generation of devices.

The fund's name encodes the thesis. "Physicians Capital" is not a description of who provides the capital. It is a statement about what physicians' insight is worth. Clinical judgment is capital. The fund exists to deploy it.

## Why Now

PhyCap launched in 2024 at a specific inflection point in healthcare venture capital:

**The physician empowerment wave is cresting.** Multiple physician-focused funds launched between 2020 and 2024 (Scrub Capital, Doctors Innovate Fund via Alumni Ventures, LOUD Capital), indicating that the market recognizes the structural exclusion of physicians from healthcare investment. PhyCap enters this wave with a sharper thesis and deeper clinical bench than any existing fund.

**Digital health investment is maturing past hype.** After a period of inflated valuations (2020-2021) and correction (2022-2023), digital health venture capital is entering a phase where clinical validation matters more than growth metrics. PhyCap's diligence model, built on actual clinical expertise rather than healthcare industry adjacency, is optimally positioned for this environment.

**Women's health is receiving unprecedented investment attention.** FemTech and women's health VC funding grew significantly through 2023-2025, driven by both market opportunity and cultural momentum. PhyCap's explicit investment priority in Women's Health and Health Inequity positions the fund to capture deals in a growing category while competitors are still adding it as an afterthought.

**The MHCDS network is mature.** Dartmouth's MHCDS program has been producing graduates since approximately 2010, creating a network of 500+ senior healthcare leaders trained in delivery science, systems thinking, and evidence-based management. Paul and Robin are not just credential holders; they are embedded in a network of decision-makers who think about healthcare the same way they do.

## Why Tulsa

PhyCap's headquarters at 12 N Cheyenne Ave, Tulsa, Oklahoma, is a deliberate choice, not a default:

**Cost structure advantage.** Operating a Fund I vehicle from Tulsa rather than Boston, San Francisco, or New York means lower overhead, which translates to more capital deployed per dollar raised. For a $10M fund, operational efficiency matters.

**Proximity to underserved markets.** PhyCap's investment thesis includes health inequity. Oklahoma and the broader central United States contain some of the most underserved healthcare markets in the country. Headquartering in Tulsa aligns the fund's physical presence with its stated mission.

**FortySix Capital partnership.** The co-management relationship with FortySix Venture Capital, also based in Tulsa, provides operational infrastructure (fund administration, compliance, back-office) that allows PhyCap's physician GPs to focus on clinical diligence and portfolio support rather than fund operations.

**Emerging innovation corridor.** Tulsa has invested heavily in attracting tech talent and remote workers (Tulsa Remote program), building a startup ecosystem that includes healthcare technology. PhyCap and FortySix together position Tulsa as a healthcare VC node outside the traditional coastal hubs.

The Tulsa narrative works best when connected to mission rather than geography. The message is not "We are based in Tulsa." The message is "We chose to build where the healthcare system's failures are most visible, not where the venture capital industry is most comfortable."

## Why These Physicians

The GP team was not assembled by recruiting physicians who wanted to try venture capital. It was assembled by identifying physicians whose clinical specialties collectively cover the full clinical lifecycle and whose professional experiences include the exact transitions (clinical practice to technology evaluation to systems-level thinking) that PhyCap's investment thesis requires.

Paul Slosar brings the spine surgery-to-CMO-to-MHCDS arc that proves a physician can operate at every level of healthcare innovation. Robin Noble brings the OB-GYN clinical authority plus MHCDS systems training that makes her the investment thesis personified. Her Columbia MD, Yale residency, Harvard Surgical Leadership certificate, and MHCDS create a credential stack that no other women's health investor in venture capital can match. Vipul Kella brings the emergency medicine urgency and DC-area policy proximity that ground the fund's technology evaluation in real-time clinical reality. Tim Martens brings the pediatric cardiothoracic credibility that signals to LPs this is not a casual physician side project. Fred Liss, Joseph Jasser, Giovanni Lorenz, Alex Tang, and Jerald Dawkins each fill specific clinical and analytical gaps in the diligence team.

The result is a GP roster that can review any healthcare technology product from every clinical angle without hiring external advisors. That capability is the fund's structural advantage, and it is not reproducible by competitors who assemble advisory boards rather than building physician-led GP teams.
