# Investment Thesis

## The Three Pillars as the Moment of Truth

PhyCap's investment thesis is not a list of categories. It is a single insight expressed through three interdependent pillars, each targeting a specific dimension of the same moment: the moment when care is delivered and clinical decisions are made.

---

### Pillar 1: Clinical Care Delivery Workflow Optimization
**The Moment**

This is the point of contact between physician and patient: the surgical procedure, the diagnostic workup, the treatment plan, the referral, the documentation. Clinical Care Delivery Workflow Optimization targets technologies that improve what happens at this moment.

**What PhyCap evaluates**: Software and tools that reduce friction in the clinical workflow. This includes documentation platforms, scheduling and coordination tools, decision support systems, referral management, and any technology that sits between the physician's intention and the patient's outcome.

**Why clinical insight matters here**: A generalist VC evaluates workflow tools by market size, competitive landscape, and growth metrics. PhyCap's GPs evaluate them by asking: Does this tool actually fit into a 7-minute primary care appointment? Will an orthopedic surgeon stop mid-procedure to interact with this interface? Does this documentation platform create more work than it eliminates? The MHCDS training Paul and Robin bring is specifically designed for this evaluation: healthcare delivery science teaches how to assess the systemic impact of process changes, not just the product's feature set.

**Exclusions within this pillar**: Administrative workflow tools that do not touch the clinical encounter (billing-only software, HR platforms for healthcare organizations). PhyCap invests at the moment of care delivery, not in the back office.

---

### Pillar 2: Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), FDA Class I/II
**The Device**

This is the tool in the physician's hands at the moment of care. Software as a Medical Device is software intended to be used for one or more medical purposes that performs these purposes without being part of a hardware medical device. PhyCap targets FDA Class I (general controls) and Class II (general controls with special controls) SaMD products.

**What PhyCap evaluates**: Diagnostic algorithms, clinical decision support software, imaging analysis tools, monitoring software, and any software product that requires or benefits from FDA clearance/authorization as a medical device.

**Why clinical insight matters here**: The FDA regulatory pathway for SaMD is complex. Class I and Class II designations carry different requirements for clinical evidence, quality management systems, and post-market surveillance. PhyCap's GP team (particularly Giovanni Lorenz in radiology, where AI-driven SaMD is most advanced, and Paul Slosar, who navigated FDA processes at Titan Spine) can evaluate not just whether a SaMD product works but whether its regulatory strategy is viable, whether the clinical evidence plan is sufficient, and whether physicians will actually adopt a software-based device into their clinical decision-making.

**Exclusions within this pillar**: Class III SaMD (high-risk, requiring PMA approval). Life sciences software. Pharmaceutical decision tools. Hardware medical devices (PhyCap invests in software, not hardware).

---

### Pillar 3: Women's Health and Health Inequity
**The Patient**

This is the patient most historically underserved at the moment of care delivery. Women's healthcare has been systematically underfunded, under-researched, and under-innovated across nearly every clinical domain: reproductive health, maternal care, menopause, cardiovascular disease presentation in women, autoimmune conditions, chronic pain, and mental health.

**What PhyCap evaluates**: Technologies addressing gaps in women's healthcare delivery, diagnostic tools calibrated for female physiology, reproductive health platforms, maternal health monitoring, menopause management solutions, and innovations targeting health inequities in underserved populations.

**Why clinical insight matters here**: Robin Noble's clinical practice in OB-GYN gives PhyCap a direct window into the daily realities of women's healthcare delivery: what is missing, what is broken, and what would actually be adopted by clinicians serving this population. Her MHCDS training adds the systems lens: she evaluates women's health technologies not just as clinical tools but as interventions within a delivery system that has structurally underserved this patient population.

**The Women's Health Nexus connection**: PhyCap's November 2025 event at Congressional Country Club (200+ attendees, law firm sponsors, American Bazaar co-host) was a category claim in this pillar. The event demonstrated that PhyCap does not just invest in women's health; it convenes the ecosystem.

---

## The Integrated Logic

The three pillars are not parallel categories. They are nested:

**The MOMENT** (Pillar 1): Clinical workflow optimization is the context. Every healthcare technology product, regardless of category, must integrate into a clinical workflow to create value.

**The DEVICE** (Pillar 2): SaMD is the instrument. Within that workflow, the software-based medical device is the specific tool PhyCap evaluates. The fund targets the intersection of software and clinical decision-making.

**The PATIENT** (Pillar 3): Women's Health is the patient population. Within the workflow, using the SaMD device, PhyCap asks: who is this technology serving? And are the most underserved populations included?

A deal that addresses all three pillars (a software-based medical device that optimizes a clinical workflow for an underserved women's health population) is the highest-value intersection of PhyCap's thesis.

## Gaps in This Section

- The "nested" logic (moment, device, patient) is a strategic frame proposed in this Brand HQ. It should be tested against the GP team's actual decision-making process. If the three pillars function more independently than the nested model suggests, the framing should be adjusted.
- Specific deal criteria within each pillar (minimum revenue, team composition, traction thresholds) are not publicly documented. If these criteria exist internally, they should inform the founder-facing messaging.
- Whether PhyCap has made investments across all three pillars or has concentrated in one is not publicly known. As the portfolio grows, the thesis framing should reflect actual investment pattern.
