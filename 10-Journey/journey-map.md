# Customer Journey Maps

## Journey 1: Physician LP Path

### Stage 1: Awareness

**Customer Mindset:**
"I keep hearing that physicians should be more involved in healthcare innovation, but I do not know what that looks like beyond writing a check to my financial advisor."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- LinkedIn post from a PhyCap GP (Paul Slosar, Dutch Rojas, or another GP) discussing a specific clinical workflow problem
- Peer mention at a medical society conference or physician-executive program alumni event
- Women's Health Nexus event coverage or invitation shared by a colleague
- STAT News or Health Affairs article referencing physician-led investment trends
- PhyCap website homepage (first visit, typically from LinkedIn click-through)

**Content Needed:**
- GP-authored LinkedIn posts that demonstrate clinical depth, not fund marketing (published 2-3x/week across GP profiles)
- One-page "Why Physician-Led VC" explainer that frames the structural problem (physicians excluded from capital decisions) and PhyCap's response
- Short video (Paul Slosar overview video currently on homepage) that establishes the thesis in under 3 minutes
- Event recaps from Women's Health Nexus and similar gatherings that showcase the community quality

**Friction Points:**
- PhyCap company LinkedIn has 1,132 followers; awareness depends heavily on GP personal profile reach, which varies significantly across the 10 GPs
- No paid distribution or media partnerships to extend content beyond organic networks
- The brand name "PhyCap" and "Physicians Capital Fund" are not self-explanatory; first-time visitors need immediate context

**Success Metrics:**
- LinkedIn company page follower growth (monthly)
- Website unique visitors from LinkedIn referral (monthly)
- Event attendance inquiries (per event)
- GP personal profile post engagement rates

---

### Stage 2: Consideration

**Customer Mindset:**
"This looks different from other physician investment groups. The team is real. The thesis is specific. I want to understand how this actually works before I talk to anyone about committing capital."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- PhyCap Resources page (8+ articles on healthcare trends, physician investing, technology adoption, healthcare economics)
- Community page (4 LP testimonials: Brian Russell, Emil Engels, Maxwell Ginsberg, James Newman)
- Team page (accordion format showing 10 GPs and their credentials)
- PhyCap LEARN page (intended as education resource)
- Peer conversation with an existing LP or GP
- Email to info@46.capital requesting more information

**Content Needed:**
- Detailed fund overview document (thesis, check size, stage focus, exclusions, team bios, timeline)
- LP FAQ: minimum commitment, fund structure, capital call schedule, expected timeline to returns, illiquidity explanation
- "What LPs Actually Do" content that explains the Innovation Circle, clinical advisory opportunities, and community engagement beyond capital commitment
- Testimonial content that goes deeper than pull quotes (longer-form LP stories about why they joined and what the experience has been)

**Friction Points:**
- **LEARN page is not publicly accessible.** This is the most significant friction point in the consideration stage. A physician who wants to self-educate before reaching out is blocked. They must either email a general inbox or send a LinkedIn message to a GP, both of which require a higher commitment threshold than browsing an educational page.
- LP testimonials on the Community page are brief pull quotes. They establish social proof but do not answer the operational questions a considering physician has.
- The accordion format on the Team page requires clicking to expand each GP's bio. A physician evaluating whether the team includes someone from their specialty must click through 10 accordions.
- No portfolio showcase beyond BioReact. A physician comparing PhyCap to Scrub Capital or Doctors Innovate Fund cannot evaluate investment track record.

**Success Metrics:**
- Resources page time-on-page and article completion rates
- Team page interaction depth (accordion expansions)
- Inbound emails to info@46.capital requesting fund information
- LP referral conversations initiated (tracked by GPs)

### Stage 3: Decision

**Customer Mindset:**
"I believe in the thesis and I trust the team. Now I need to understand the mechanics, discuss it with my spouse/partner, and decide whether $150K-$500K in an illiquid vehicle is the right allocation for us right now."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- One-on-one conversation with a GP (ideally one who shares the prospect's specialty or adjacent clinical experience)
- PhyCap INVEST page (intended as commitment pathway)
- Fund documentation (PPM, subscription agreement, LP agreement)
- Conversation with the prospect's financial advisor or wealth manager
- Conversation with spouse/partner about the commitment

**Content Needed:**
- Fund mechanics one-pager designed for non-physician household members (explains VC structure, J-curve, illiquidity, timeline in plain language)
- Financial advisor briefing document (so the prospect's wealth manager can evaluate PhyCap within the context of their overall portfolio)
- Clear commitment process documentation: steps from "I want to invest" to "I am an LP"
- GP-to-prospect matching protocol: connecting the prospect with the GP most relevant to their specialty for a peer conversation

**Friction Points:**
- **INVEST page is not publicly accessible.** A physician who has decided they want to commit cannot find the process on the website. This forces all conversion through direct GP outreach or the general inbox, creating bottlenecks.
- No clear next step from the website. The CTA landscape includes multiple competing calls-to-action without a single, obvious "Start Here" path for prospective LPs.
- Financial advisor gatekeeping: many physicians rely on advisors who may be unfamiliar with or skeptical of physician-led VC as an asset class
- The $150K minimum is significant for early-career physicians or those with high student debt loads, even if they are technically accredited investors

**Success Metrics:**
- GP-to-prospect conversation conversion rate (meeting held to commitment made)
- Time from first conversation to signed subscription agreement
- Commitment size distribution (are most LPs at $150K minimum or higher?)
- Financial advisor objection frequency and type

### Stage 4: Onboarding

**Customer Mindset:**
"I have committed capital. Now show me that this is more than a financial transaction. I want to understand how I engage, what I have access to, and how my clinical expertise contributes."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Welcome communication (email, welcome packet)
- Innovation Circle introduction and onboarding
- First LP update (fund activity, pipeline, portfolio company introductions)
- Introduction to other LPs in similar specialties
- Access to community events and programming

**Content Needed:**
- LP onboarding guide: what to expect in Year 1, how capital calls work, how to access Innovation Circle programming, how to contribute clinical expertise to portfolio evaluation
- Innovation Circle calendar and programming overview
- Portfolio company introductions with clinical context (not just company name and sector, but "here is why this matters for your specialty")
- LP directory or networking mechanism that enables peer connection

**Friction Points:**
- If onboarding is purely transactional (signed documents, capital call, silence), the LP's emotional engagement drops immediately. The gap between "I joined to shape the future of medicine" and "I received a K-1" is where retention risk lives.
- Innovation Circle programming cadence and quality is not documented publicly; new LPs may be uncertain about what they actually joined beyond the fund
- GPs with active clinical practices have limited bandwidth for LP relationship management

**Success Metrics:**
- Innovation Circle event attendance by new LPs (first 90 days)
- LP satisfaction survey (if conducted)
- Unsolicited LP engagement (deal referrals, clinical advisory offers, event attendance)

### Stage 5: Retention

**Customer Mindset:**
"I need to see that my participation matters. Show me how clinical expertise shaped a deal decision, how the portfolio is performing, and why re-upping in Fund II is worth it."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Quarterly LP updates (portfolio performance, new investments, pipeline)
- Annual LP meeting or event
- Innovation Circle ongoing programming
- GP-to-LP communication about specific deals where clinical expertise was relevant
- Portfolio company updates that demonstrate clinical impact

**Content Needed:**
- Quarterly LP letters that go beyond financial metrics to describe how clinical evaluation shaped deal selection and portfolio company support
- Case studies: "How physician input changed the trajectory of [portfolio company]"
- Fund II preview and early commitment opportunity for existing LPs
- LP spotlight content that recognizes individual LP contributions to the community

**Friction Points:**
- With only BioReact publicly confirmed, portfolio performance storytelling is limited in Fund I's early stages
- LP expectations for "active role in shaping the future of medicine" must be met with concrete engagement opportunities, not just reporting
- If GPs are overwhelmed with clinical practice, fund management, and deal sourcing, LP relationship management may be deprioritized

**Success Metrics:**
- LP retention rate for Fund II (percentage of Fund I LPs who re-commit)
- LP referral rate (number of qualified prospects referred by existing LPs)
- Innovation Circle engagement depth (event attendance, deal referrals, clinical advisory participation)
- LP NPS or satisfaction scores

---

### Stage 6: Advocacy

**Customer Mindset:**
"I tell colleagues about PhyCap because I believe in what we are building together. This is not just an investment; it is a movement I want to be part of growing."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Peer conversations at medical society events and hospital settings
- LinkedIn sharing and commenting on PhyCap content
- Event co-hosting or speaking at PhyCap gatherings
- Introduction of qualified physician prospects to GPs
- Testimonial contribution (joining Brian Russell, Emil Engels, Maxwell Ginsberg, James Newman)

**Content Needed:**
- Shareable LP stories and testimonial content that LPs can forward to colleagues
- "Bring a Colleague" event invitations that give existing LPs a mechanism to introduce prospects
- LP referral program structure (if formal) or at minimum, clear communication that referrals are valued
- Co-branded content opportunities for LPs who want to write or speak about their experience

**Friction Points:**
- Physician advocates need a clear, concise way to explain PhyCap to colleagues. If the value proposition requires a 15-minute explanation, referrals will be limited to close relationships.
- Compliance considerations: physicians in certain institutional settings may face restrictions on promoting investment products to colleagues
- Without a publicly accessible LEARN or INVEST page, even enthusiastic advocates cannot point prospects to a self-serve information resource

**Success Metrics:**
- Referral-originated LP commitments as percentage of total
- LP testimonial willingness (number of LPs who agree to provide testimonials)
- LinkedIn content sharing by LPs (reshares, comments on PhyCap and GP posts)
- Event attendance driven by LP invitations

## Journey 2: Healthcare Founder Path

### Stage 1: Awareness

**Customer Mindset:**
"I need investors who understand healthcare, not investors I have to educate about FDA pathways and clinical workflows for 30 minutes before I can pitch my company."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- LinkedIn post from a PhyCap GP discussing a clinical problem the founder's product addresses
- Women's Health Nexus event (for women's health/FemTech founders)
- Referral from another healthcare VC who co-invests with PhyCap/FortySix
- Healthcare startup accelerator or incubator network
- PhyCap website homepage and thesis description
- Physician advisor or clinical collaborator who mentions PhyCap

**Content Needed:**
- GP-authored content on specific clinical domains (Robin Noble on women's health access, Vipul Kella on emergency department workflow, Giovanni Lorenz on radiology AI) that signals thesis-level depth
- PhyCap investment thesis page that clearly states: check size ($150K-$500K), stage (Seed-Series A), focus areas, and exclusions (no life sciences, no pharma)
- Portfolio company profiles that demonstrate the type of company PhyCap invests in (limited to BioReact currently)

**Friction Points:**
- PhyCap's brand awareness among healthcare founders is limited compared to established healthcare VCs (LRVHealth, Rock Health, Catalyst Health Ventures)
- The $150K-$500K check size may not be visible enough in the market for founders seeking larger lead checks
- No dedicated "For Founders" landing page that immediately communicates thesis, process, and team

**Success Metrics:**
- Inbound pitch inquiries (monthly volume)
- GP content engagement from founder profiles (LinkedIn)
- Event attendance by healthcare founders
- Referral sources for deal flow (tracking how founders find PhyCap)

---

### Stage 2: Consideration

**Customer Mindset:**
"The team has the right specialties. The thesis aligns. But can a Fund I with $10M actually help me, or will they take a board seat and then not have the resources to support me?"

**Key Touchpoints:**
- PhyCap Team page (evaluating which GPs have relevant clinical expertise)
- PhyCap PITCH page (intended as founder intake pathway)
- Conversations with existing portfolio company founders
- FortySix Venture Capital relationship and co-investment structure
- GP LinkedIn profiles (evaluating depth of clinical expertise and engagement)

**Content Needed:**
- "How We Evaluate" content that explains PhyCap's clinical due diligence process (not just financial due diligence but how GP clinical expertise shapes deal evaluation)
- Founder testimonials or case studies from portfolio companies (what the GP engagement actually looks like post-investment)
- Clear explanation of the FortySix Venture Capital co-management relationship (who leads, who supports, how decisions are made)
- GP specialty mapping: which GP evaluates deals in which clinical domain

**Friction Points:**
- **PITCH page is not publicly accessible.** Founders who want to submit a pitch must email info@46.capital or message GPs directly on LinkedIn, which creates an unstructured intake process.
- No publicly visible portfolio beyond BioReact limits the founder's ability to evaluate PhyCap's track record and post-investment support quality
- Fund I size ($10M) raises legitimate questions about follow-on capacity and signaling risk if PhyCap cannot participate in subsequent rounds
- The FortySix co-management relationship may be confusing: is the founder's primary relationship with PhyCap or FortySix?

**Success Metrics:**
- PITCH page visits (once accessible) or inbound pitch email volume
- Founder-to-GP conversation conversion rate
- Time from first contact to first meeting
- Founder feedback on pitch process experience

---

### Stage 3: Decision

**Customer Mindset:**
"I want PhyCap on my cap table. I need to understand the terms, the GP engagement commitment, and how the physician network will actually support my clinical validation and market access."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Term sheet and investment agreement review
- Deep-dive meeting with the GP(s) most relevant to the founder's clinical domain
- Reference calls with existing portfolio company founders
- Co-investment discussions with FortySix Venture Capital and other round participants
- Legal and regulatory review (particularly for SaMD companies with FDA considerations)

**Content Needed:**
- Term sheet standards and investment process timeline
- Post-investment GP engagement model documentation (how many hours per month, what kind of support, who is the primary point of contact)
- Innovation Circle access description: how can portfolio companies leverage the physician network for clinical validation, pilot partnerships, and advisory input
- Clear articulation of what PhyCap brings beyond capital (physician network, clinical credibility for health system sales, FDA pathway input from clinically-informed investors)

**Friction Points:**
- Decision timeline may conflict with founder urgency; if GPs are in surgery or on clinical rotations, response times may lag behind generalist VC firms
- Fund I size means PhyCap is rarely the lead investor; founders must coordinate PhyCap's participation with a lead who sets terms
- No established pattern of co-investment cadence with specific funds (beyond FortySix) that would give founders confidence in round completion

**Success Metrics:**
- Term sheet to close conversion rate
- Average time from term sheet to wire
- Founder satisfaction with process (survey or informal feedback)
- Number of deals lost to competing funds and reasons cited

---

### Stage 4: Onboarding

**Customer Mindset:**
"Capital is in the bank. Now I need to know exactly how to activate the physician network for clinical validation, pilot introductions, and advisory support."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Post-investment kickoff meeting with assigned GP(s)
- Introduction to Innovation Circle and relevant physician contacts
- Quarterly reporting cadence and expectations established
- Portfolio company community introduction (connections to other PhyCap portfolio companies)
- FortySix operational support introduction

**Content Needed:**
- Portfolio company onboarding guide: reporting requirements, GP engagement model, Innovation Circle access protocols, communication cadence
- GP-to-portfolio-company matching document: which GPs bring which clinical networks, advisory capabilities, and health system relationships
- Innovation Circle engagement playbook for portfolio companies: how to request clinical advisory input, pilot introductions, and clinical validation support
- Quarterly reporting template that captures both financial and clinical progress metrics

**Friction Points:**
- If the GP assigned to the portfolio company is in a different specialty than the company's clinical domain, the match may feel superficial
- Innovation Circle physician engagement is voluntary; portfolio companies cannot guarantee physician participation in advisory or pilot discussions
- Founder expectations for GP engagement hours may exceed what practicing physicians can deliver

**Success Metrics:**
- Number of GP-facilitated introductions per portfolio company (first 90 days)
- Innovation Circle physician advisory sessions initiated per portfolio company
- Portfolio company founder satisfaction with onboarding process
- Time from investment close to first meaningful GP engagement

---

### Stage 5: Retention

**Customer Mindset:**
"PhyCap's physician network has opened doors that would have taken me 18 months to find on my own. I want to make sure this support continues through my next fundraise."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Ongoing GP advisory engagement (monthly or quarterly check-ins)
- Follow-on investment discussions for subsequent rounds
- Health system introduction facilitation
- Clinical validation and evidence generation support
- Portfolio company events and peer networking

**Content Needed:**
- Portfolio company progress updates shared with Innovation Circle (with founder permission) to maintain physician network engagement
- Follow-on investment policy documentation: under what conditions does PhyCap participate in Series A/B, and what are the constraints of Fund I's reserve strategy
- Health system introduction process: how GPs facilitate warm introductions to procurement and clinical leadership contacts
- Clinical evidence generation guidance: how PhyCap's physician network can support clinical trial design, IRB navigation, and peer-reviewed publication strategy

**Friction Points:**
- Fund I reserve capacity ($10M total fund) limits follow-on investment ability, which may frustrate founders who want PhyCap to maintain pro-rata
- GP bandwidth constraints: as the portfolio grows, each GP's time is divided across more portfolio companies
- Physician network introductions depend on individual GP relationships, which may be concentrated in certain health systems or geographies

**Success Metrics:**
- Follow-on investment participation rate
- GP-facilitated health system meetings per portfolio company (annually)
- Portfolio company milestone achievement (FDA clearance, revenue milestones, subsequent fundraising)
- Founder NPS for PhyCap GP engagement

---

### Stage 6: Advocacy

**Customer Mindset:**
"I tell every healthcare founder I know that they need physician investors, and PhyCap is the real version of that. The clinical engagement is not a pitch deck slide; it is how they actually operate."

**Key Touchpoints:**
- Founder referrals of other healthcare startups to PhyCap
- Speaking at PhyCap events about the portfolio company experience
- Co-authored content with PhyCap GPs about clinical innovation
- Public endorsement of PhyCap's model in healthcare media and at conferences
- Participation in Women's Health Nexus and similar events as a portfolio company showcase

**Content Needed:**
- Portfolio company showcase format for events and website (once portfolio expands beyond BioReact)
- Co-branded content opportunities: GP + founder co-authored articles about clinical innovation in specific domains
- Founder referral recognition and tracking system
- Case study format that highlights how physician-led evaluation and support differentiated the PhyCap experience

**Friction Points:**
- Founders may be reluctant to publicly endorse a Fund I without demonstrated returns
- Co-authored content requires coordination between busy founders and busy physician GPs
- Competitive dynamics: founders may not want to publicly signal their investor relationships to competing startups

**Success Metrics:**
- Founder-referred deal flow as percentage of total pipeline
- Portfolio company participation in PhyCap events
- Co-authored content published (GP + founder)
- Media mentions citing PhyCap portfolio companies

## Cross-Journey Broken Paths Summary

| Broken Path | Journey Affected | Stage | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEARN page not accessible | Physician LP | Consideration | Blocks self-serve education; forces all learning through direct GP contact |
| INVEST page not accessible | Physician LP | Decision | Blocks commitment pathway; creates bottleneck at info@46.capital inbox |
| PITCH page not accessible | Healthcare Founder | Consideration | Blocks structured deal flow intake; forces unstructured inbound |
| Multiple competing CTAs | Both | All stages | Dilutes user intent; no single clear path for either audience |
| No portfolio showcase | Both | Consideration, Decision | Limits social proof and track record evaluation |
| Contact through info@46.capital only | Both | Consideration, Decision | General inbox creates uncertainty about response time and recipient |

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No actual conversion data informs these journey maps.** The touchpoints, friction points, and success metrics are constructed from website analysis, content review, and healthcare professional behavior patterns. Actual LP and founder journey data would validate or revise these maps.
2. **The FortySix Venture Capital handoff points are undefined.** At what stages does the founder interact with FortySix versus PhyCap? This co-management relationship creates potential confusion points that are not mapped.
3. **Innovation Circle engagement model is assumed, not documented.** The journey maps reference Innovation Circle programming as a key touchpoint, but the actual programming cadence, content, and engagement mechanisms are not publicly visible.
4. **No journey mapping for unsuccessful conversions.** Why do prospects who enter the consideration stage fail to convert? Exit interview or survey data from prospects who chose not to invest or founders who chose a different fund would reveal the most critical friction points.
5. **Time-in-stage benchmarks not established.** How long should each stage take? Without benchmarks, it is impossible to identify where the journey is stalling.
