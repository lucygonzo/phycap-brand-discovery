## The Clinical Insight That Makes Him Dangerous as an Investor

Paul Slosar has operated at the intersection of three worlds that rarely overlap in a single person: the operating room, the C-suite, and the delivery science classroom.

As a spine surgeon, he spent years inside the most technically demanding and equipment-intensive procedures in orthopedics. He saw firsthand where devices worked, where they failed, where workflow friction added time and risk, and where the OR team compensated for system failures with heroic individual effort.

As CMO of Titan Spine, he crossed from the clinical side to the industry side. He saw how medical device companies build, market, and sell products to the surgeons who use them. He understood the regulatory pathway, the reimbursement landscape, and the gap between what engineers build and what clinicians actually need in the moment of care delivery.

As an MHCDS graduate, he added the systems layer. The Dartmouth program (Tuck Business + Geisel Medicine, 12 months of interdisciplinary study) trains clinicians to see healthcare as a system of interlocking processes, data flows, and incentive structures. Paul can diagnose a clinical workflow the way a systems engineer diagnoses a manufacturing line: identifying bottlenecks, measuring variation, and designing interventions that improve outcomes at the population level, not just the individual patient level.

This combination is unique in healthcare venture capital. Other physician-investors bring clinical credibility. Paul brings clinical credibility plus systems-level analytical training plus industry operating experience. He does not evaluate startups from the outside. He evaluates them from the perspective of someone who has been the surgeon, the CMO, and the delivery scientist.

**The MHCDS moat:** No competing physician-led VC fund has a GP with the MHCDS credential. This is not a minor differentiator. The MHCDS produces approximately 45 graduates per year from a program that explicitly trains physicians to bridge clinical practice and healthcare system design. Paul's thesis for PhyCap (clinical care delivery workflow optimization) is a direct intellectual descendant of his MHCDS training. The credential is not decorative. It is the analytical foundation of the fund's investment approach.

## The Tension He Lives In

Paul operates in a productive tension between two modes of thinking:

**Clinical precision:** The surgeon's mindset. Specific, procedural, outcome-driven. Every variable matters. Risk is managed through preparation, technique, and evidence. Success is measured in individual patient outcomes.

**Systems-level investment thinking:** The delivery scientist's mindset. Aggregate, structural, population-driven. Individual variables matter less than systemic patterns. Risk is managed through portfolio construction, thesis discipline, and market timing. Success is measured in scalable impact.

This tension is the engine of Paul's content. His most compelling ideas emerge when he holds both perspectives simultaneously: seeing a startup through the surgeon's eyes (will this work in the OR?) and through the systems thinker's eyes (will this scale across the delivery system?).

Content that flattens this tension into one dimension (pure clinical or pure investor) loses what makes Paul's voice distinctive. The goal is to let both perspectives coexist in every post.

## Natural Voice

Paul's voice is:
- **Authoritative without being authoritarian.** He presents his perspective as informed by deep experience, not as the only valid view. He invites engagement rather than shutting down debate.
- **Evidence-based.** He references data, studies, and observed patterns rather than making sweeping claims. When he offers an opinion, he shows his work.
- **Systems-thinking.** He naturally connects individual observations to structural patterns. A single clinical example becomes a lens on the entire delivery system.
- **Measured.** He does not use hyperbole, urgency language, or breathless excitement. His credibility comes from being the calmest, most precise voice in the conversation.
- **Intellectually generous.** He explains complex ideas clearly without condescending. He assumes his audience is smart but may not share his specific vantage point.

**Voice guardrails:**
- Never casual or colloquial (no "game-changer," "disruption," "crushing it")
- Never dismissive of incumbents or traditional healthcare (he came from that world)
- Never defensive about physician-led investing (confidence, not defensiveness)
- Always specific (name the workflow, name the failure mode, name the data point)

## Best LinkedIn Format

**Primary format:** Long-form text posts (1,200 to 1,800 characters) with a clear thesis, supporting evidence, and a closing question or observation that invites commentary.

**Structure that works for Paul:**
1. Opening line: A specific observation or data point (not a question or provocative hook)
2. Context: Why this matters for healthcare delivery
3. Analysis: Paul's systems-level interpretation
4. PhyCap lens: How this connects to the investment thesis
5. Closing: A question for the audience or a forward-looking implication

**Secondary format:** Carousel posts (4 to 6 slides) for frameworks, comparisons, or multi-step arguments that benefit from visual structure.

**Avoid:** Short hot-take posts, selfies, personal anecdotes without analytical payoff, polls.

**Cadence:** 3x per week. Suggested rhythm: Monday (thesis-driven), Wednesday (industry analysis), Friday (MHCDS/delivery science or healthcare systems).
