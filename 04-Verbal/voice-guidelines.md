# Voice Guidelines

## The North Star: NEJM Format Meets Equinox Energy

PhyCap's voice occupies a specific register: the authority of a peer-reviewed medical journal combined with the desirability of a premium membership brand. This combination produces content that physicians take seriously (because it reads like the publications they trust) and find aspirational (because it signals insider status in a way that feels earned rather than purchased).

## Tone Attributes

### 1. Evidence-Forward
**Do**: Lead with data, specific numbers, named conditions, and cited sources. Present claims with the same rigor a physician would expect in a clinical paper. "PhyCap screens 500+ deals annually across three thesis areas" is evidence-forward. "PhyCap is highly selective" is not.

**Don't**: Make unsubstantiated claims. Use superlatives without data ("the best," "the most," "the leading"). Present opinions as facts. Use vague quantifiers ("many," "significant," "substantial").

**Example**:
- Yes: "Fund I targets Seed through Series A companies with check sizes of $150K to $500K, focused on three investment priorities where clinical insight creates measurable diligence advantage."
- No: "We invest in the most promising healthcare startups that are transforming the industry."

### 2. Clinically Grounded
**Do**: Reference specific clinical realities, named specialties, and real-world practice patterns. Use medical language naturally, not performatively. When discussing technology, frame it in terms of the clinical workflow it enters.

**Don't**: Use medical terminology as decoration. Reference "the healthcare system" in the abstract without specificity. Over-explain clinical concepts to a physician audience (they already know).

**Example**:
- Yes: "When a workflow optimization platform claims to reduce physician documentation time, our diligence process tests that claim against the documentation patterns of six different specialties."
- No: "We understand the healthcare challenges facing physicians today."

### 3. Understated Confidence
**Do**: State PhyCap's differentiators as facts, not boasts. Let the MHCDS credential, the 168+ years of experience, and the 13+ specialty coverage speak for themselves. The confidence comes from specificity, not volume.

**Don't**: Use exclamation points. Use language that sounds like pitching ("Join now!", "Don't miss this opportunity!"). Over-claim. PhyCap's position is strong enough that understating it actually communicates more confidence than overselling it.

**Example**:
- Yes: "Two of PhyCap's GPs hold Dartmouth's Master of Health Care Delivery Science. No other physician-led fund has this credential."
- No: "PhyCap has an incredible, industry-leading team with unmatched credentials!"

### 4. Collegial, Not Hierarchical
**Do**: Use "we" to include the LP community. Address physician readers as peers, not prospects. Reference shared experience ("as clinicians," "in practice"). The relationship is collaborative.

**Don't**: Use language that positions PhyCap above its audience ("we invite you," "we offer you access," "we empower you"). Physicians do not want to be empowered by a fund. They want to be recognized as equals who bring something valuable.

**Example**:
- Yes: "PhyCap exists because physicians asked a question the venture capital industry had not answered: who evaluates healthcare technology better than the clinicians who use it?"
- No: "We empower physicians to take an active role in healthcare innovation."

### 5. Specific, Not Abstract
**Do**: Name the specialty. Name the clinical scenario. Name the GP who would review it. The more specific the reference, the more credible the voice.

**Don't**: Abstract to "healthcare" when you mean "surgical workflows." Abstract to "innovation" when you mean "FDA Class II SaMD products." Abstract to "the future" when you mean "women's health diagnostics for menopause management."

## Sentence Structure Rules

**Vary sentence length.** Alternate between short declarative sentences (for impact) and longer explanatory sentences (for depth). Avoid monotonous rhythm.

**Lead with the claim, follow with the evidence.** PhyCap's voice does not build suspense. It states the point, then supports it.

**No em dashes.** This is a confirmed brand rule. Restructure any sentence that would use an em dash. Use a period and a new sentence. Use a colon. Use parentheses for brief asides.

**Avoid the "Not X, it's Y" construction.** Do not write "This is not about investment returns. This is about physician agency." Restructure: "Physician agency drives this fund. Returns follow from the same insight."

## Citation and Data Presentation

**In articles and thought leadership**: Reference specific data points with attribution. "According to Rock Health's 2025 Digital Health Funding Report, digital health raised $X.XB." Physicians are trained to evaluate sources. Give them sources.

**In social posts**: Single stat with context. "168+ combined years of clinical experience. 13+ specialties. One diligence process." The stat does the work. The context frames it.

**In LP-facing materials**: Data tables, named metrics, specific performance language (within regulatory constraints). Physicians read lab reports. Format LP data the way they already process information.

## Community Language

**Innovation Circle**: Use this name for the community. The community is a "Circle" (insider, peer-level) not a "network" (generic) or "club" (exclusive for its own sake).

**Member language**: "Members" or "community members" for free tier. "Investors" or "LP members" for the investment tier. Never "customers" or "clients."

**Event language**: "Convening" rather than "conference." "Session" rather than "panel." "Working dinner" rather than "networking event." PhyCap events produce outcomes, not connections.
