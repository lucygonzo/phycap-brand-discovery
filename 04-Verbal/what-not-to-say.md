# What Not to Say

## Language Retirement List

Each entry includes the phrase to retire, why it fails the PhyCap standard, and the replacement direction.

---

### 1. "More than just a Venture Capital Fund"
**Where it appears**: Positioning statement on homepage and Community page.
**Why retire**: Every fund says some version of this. The "more than just" construction concedes the baseline ("we are a VC fund") and then vaguely gestures at something additional without naming it. The phrase does no work. It fills space where a specific claim should be.
**Replacement direction**: State what PhyCap is, specifically. "PhyCap is a physician-led venture capital fund that invests at the moment of truth in medicine." No "more than." No qualifier. The specificity is the differentiator.

### 2. "Transformative initiative"
**Where it appears**: Positioning statement, homepage.
**Why retire**: "Transformative" is one of the most overused words in healthcare and venture capital. It communicates nothing because everything claims to be transformative. "Initiative" is passive; it suggests a project or program, not a fund with a thesis and capital to deploy.
**Replacement direction**: Replace with concrete language about what PhyCap transforms. "PhyCap transforms how healthcare technology is evaluated by putting clinicians at the center of the diligence process." The verb "transforms" works only when the object is named.

### 3. "Shaping the future of healthcare"
**Where it appears**: Positioning statement, mission statement, multiple pages.
**Why retire**: Every healthcare company, hospital system, insurer, and health tech startup claims to shape the future of healthcare. The phrase is so widely used that it communicates nothing about PhyCap specifically. It also positions PhyCap's impact in the future rather than the present, which undermines urgency.
**Replacement direction**: Name which future, for which patients, through which mechanism. "Investing in technologies that improve clinical workflows, software-based medical devices, and women's health outcomes." Specific. Present tense. Actionable.

### 4. "Connected ecosystem of successful healthcare experts"
**Where it appears**: Positioning statement.
**Why retire**: "Connected ecosystem" is generic community language that could describe any healthcare network, professional association, or LinkedIn group. "Successful" is a subjective qualifier that weakens rather than strengthens. "Healthcare experts" is vague when you could name surgeons, OB-GYNs, and emergency physicians.
**Replacement direction**: Name the community and what makes it distinct. "A diligence team of ten physicians spanning 13+ specialties, from pediatric cardiothoracic surgery to emergency medicine." Specificity replaces the generic.

### 5. "Dedicated to"
**Where it appears**: Multiple instances across the site.
**Why retire**: "Dedicated to" is a passive construction that says "we care about this" without saying "we do this." Dedication is an intention, not an action.
**Replacement direction**: Replace with active verbs. "Dedicated to improving physician workflows" becomes "Invests in technologies that improve physician workflows." The fund does not dedicate. It invests.

### 6. "Empower medical professionals"
**Where it appears**: Mission statement, homepage.
**Why retire**: "Empower" implies a transfer of power from PhyCap to physicians. This positions PhyCap as the agent and physicians as the recipients, which contradicts the insider precision archetype. Physicians already have the clinical insight. PhyCap structures it, not bestows it.
**Replacement direction**: "Recognize" or "structure." "PhyCap recognizes that clinical insight has investment-grade value and structures it into a diligence process no generalist fund can replicate." The power already belongs to the physicians. PhyCap creates the mechanism.

### 7. "Bridging the gap between tech innovators and those on the healthcare frontline"
**Where it appears**: Homepage.
**Why retire**: "Bridging the gap" positions PhyCap as infrastructure (a bridge) rather than an active participant. The metaphor makes PhyCap passive: it connects two sides rather than being one of them. PhyCap is not the bridge. PhyCap is the clinician in the room.
**Replacement direction**: "PhyCap brings clinical diligence to healthcare technology investment. Our GPs are the frontline physicians who evaluate whether innovation will survive contact with actual clinical practice."

### 8. "Invite" / "invited into"
**Where it appears**: "Physicians and healthcare professionals rarely get invited into venture capital" (LinkedIn about).
**Why retire partially**: The original framing is effective as a problem statement (physicians are excluded from VC). The word "invited" becomes problematic only when PhyCap positions itself as the one doing the inviting. PhyCap should not "invite" physicians to invest. It should "recognize" physicians as qualified.
**Replacement direction**: Keep the problem statement. Change the solution framing. "Physicians have rarely had a seat at the venture capital table. PhyCap puts clinicians at the center of the investment decision."

### 9. "Driving a new standard"
**Where it appears**: Homepage.
**Why retire**: "Driving a new standard" is aspirational without being specific. What is the standard? New compared to what? "Driving" is a cliche in corporate messaging.
**Replacement direction**: Name the standard. "PhyCap's diligence process evaluates every deal from 13+ clinical perspectives. That is the standard."

### 10. "The future of"
**Where it appears**: Multiple references to "the future of healthcare," "the future of medicine."
**Why retire**: Overuse. Every healthcare entity claims to represent the future. PhyCap's strength is in the present: the clinical insight that exists now, the physicians who practice now, the diligence that happens now.
**Replacement direction**: Replace future-oriented claims with present-tense specificity. "PhyCap invests in what works in clinical practice today" rather than "shaping the future of healthcare."

---

## The Universal Rule

If a phrase could be copied onto any healthcare fund's website without modification, it is not specific enough for PhyCap. Every sentence should contain at least one element that only PhyCap can claim: a named credential, a specific number, a clinical specialty, or a process detail.
