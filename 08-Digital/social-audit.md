## LinkedIn Company Page

### Current State

- **Followers:** 1,132
- **Post Frequency:** Regular (appears to be 2-4x per week based on content volume)
- **Engagement Range:** 6-38 reactions per post
- **Content Types:** Fund thesis content, healthcare trend commentary, event promotion (Women's Health Nexus), GP spotlight content, physician investing education, women's health commentary
- **Tone:** Professional, clinically informed, avoids hype language. Consistent with the "Insider Precision" brand archetype.

### What Works

- Posting cadence is strong for a Fund I with 1,132 followers. Consistency signals organizational seriousness.
- Content reflects the three thesis areas (workflow optimization, SaMD, women's health) rather than chasing broad healthcare news cycles.
- The engagement range (6-38 reactions) is reasonable for the follower base. A 0.5-3.4% engagement rate on a niche B2B page is within healthy range.
- Event content from Women's Health Nexus (200+ attendees, Congressional Country Club) provides high-quality visual and social proof content.

### What Does Not Work

- **1,132 followers is a brand awareness constraint.** For context, Scrub Capital's broader clinician community approach likely generates higher follower counts. However, follower quality matters more than quantity for PhyCap's LP and founder audiences.
- **Engagement ceiling appears to be ~38 reactions.** This suggests the content is reaching the existing network but not breaking through to new audiences. No evidence of viral or high-reach posts that significantly exceeded this ceiling.
- **No visible employee advocacy program.** With 10 GPs, a co-founder, and additional team members, the company page's reach could be amplified 5-10x through coordinated GP engagement (likes, comments, reshares on the company page posts).
- **Comment quality is more important than reaction count, but comment depth is unknown.** Are the 6-38 reactions accompanied by substantive comments from physicians and founders, or are they surface-level engagement?
- **No visible paid promotion strategy.** LinkedIn Sponsored Content targeting physicians, healthcare executives, and healthcare founders would extend reach beyond organic networks, but there is no evidence of paid amplification.

### Recommendations

1. **Establish GP engagement protocol:** Each GP commits to engaging with (liking, commenting on) 2-3 company page posts per week. This alone could double or triple organic reach.
2. **Track engagement-to-website conversion:** Are LinkedIn visitors clicking through to the website? If so, which pages? If they are clicking LEARN/INVEST/PITCH and finding nothing, the LinkedIn content is generating demand that the website cannot capture.
3. **Test post formats:** Long-form GP-authored posts (first-person clinical narratives) typically outperform link-sharing posts on LinkedIn. The GP voices are PhyCap's strongest content asset.
4. **Add follower growth target:** Set a 90-day target of 1,500 followers, driven by GP engagement amplification and event-driven content spikes.
5. **Consider LinkedIn Newsletter:** The platform's newsletter feature provides direct subscriber notification, bypassing the algorithm. A monthly "Clinical Precision in Venture" newsletter from Paul Slosar or rotating GPs would build a subscriber base independent of company page follower count.

## GP Personal LinkedIn Profiles

### Current State

**High Activity:**
- **Paul Slosar MD MHCDS (Lead GP):** Posts regularly about physician-led investing, healthcare systems, and fund thesis. His personal brand is closely tied to PhyCap's brand. Strong credibility signals: MD, MHCDS, clinical practice combined with investment perspective.
- **Dutch Rojas (Co-Founder):** Posts regularly about venture capital, healthcare innovation, and community building. Brings the operational/business perspective that complements the physician voices.

**Variable Activity:**
- **Robin Noble MD MHCDS:** Activity level not fully characterized but critical for women's health thesis content. As the OB-GYN on the GP team, Robin's voice is directly relevant to a key thesis area and the Women's Health Nexus event brand.
- **Vipul Kella MD MBA, Tim Martens MD PhD, Fred Liss MD, Joseph Jasser MD MBA, Jerald Dawkins PhD, Alex Tang PA-C MBA, Giovanni Lorenz MD:** Activity levels vary. Some may post infrequently or not at all on LinkedIn.

### Strategic Assessment

The GP personal profiles are PhyCap's most powerful distribution channel, but only if they are active. LinkedIn's algorithm prioritizes personal profiles over company pages by a factor of 5-10x in reach. A single post from Paul Slosar about a clinical workflow problem likely reaches more people than three company page posts combined.

**The imbalance is the risk.** If Paul and Dutch are the only consistent posters, PhyCap's social presence is concentrated in two voices. This creates key-person risk (if they reduce posting, reach drops) and limits the specialty breadth that should be PhyCap's content advantage (10 GPs across 13 specialties should produce content from 10 clinical perspectives, not 2).

### Recommendations

1. **Activate 4-6 GP voices beyond Paul and Dutch.** Not every GP needs to post weekly. A realistic target: each GP publishes one substantive LinkedIn post per month in their clinical domain. That produces 10 GP posts per month across diverse specialties.
2. **Create a GP Content Playbook** (see content-strategy.md) with templates, topic prompts, and examples that lower the friction for GPs who want to post but do not know what to write.
3. **Prioritize Robin Noble's LinkedIn presence.** Her OB-GYN perspective is directly tied to PhyCap's women's health thesis and Women's Health Nexus event. Content from Robin about women's health investing, cervical screening access, maternal health technology, or FemTech due diligence would be highly differentiated.
4. **Prioritize Giovanni Lorenz's LinkedIn presence.** Radiology AI is one of the most active areas in SaMD investing. A radiologist GP posting about AI diagnostic imaging from a clinical practitioner's perspective (not a tech optimist's perspective) would be distinctive.
5. **Create a "GP of the Month" content spotlight** on the company page that drives traffic to individual GP profiles, establishing each GP as a thought leader in their domain.

## X/Twitter

### Current State

- Listed but less active than LinkedIn
- No evidence of a consistent posting strategy
- Medical Twitter (#MedTwitter) is an active community, but engagement requires consistent participation to build credibility

### Strategic Assessment

X/Twitter is a secondary channel for PhyCap. The audience overlap with LinkedIn is partial: some academic physicians and healthcare founders are more active on X than LinkedIn, particularly those engaged in clinical research, health policy, and medical education discussions. However, the platform's declining stability and PhyCap's limited bandwidth make it a lower priority than LinkedIn optimization.

### Recommendations

1. **Do not invest in X/Twitter growth as a separate strategy.** The ROI for a Fund I with limited marketing resources is better allocated to LinkedIn.
2. **Cross-post selectively:** When a GP publishes a high-performing LinkedIn post, adapt it for X with appropriate hashtags (#MedTwitter, #HealthcareVC, #PhysicianInvestor, #WomensHealth, #SaMD).
3. **Monitor X for relevant conversations:** GPs should follow and occasionally engage with healthcare VC and physician investing discussions on X, even without a formal posting strategy.
4. **Reassess in 12 months:** If X stabilizes and healthcare VC discourse remains active there, consider a more intentional presence.

## YouTube

### Current State

- Used for video hosting (Paul Slosar overview video embedded on website)
- No evidence of a YouTube channel strategy (subscriber building, regular uploads, playlist organization, community engagement)
- Video content exists but is not discoverable through YouTube search or recommendations

### Strategic Assessment

YouTube is currently a utility (video hosting), not a channel (audience building). This is appropriate for PhyCap's current stage and resources. However, the video format is powerful for physician audiences accustomed to grand rounds, CME lectures, and conference presentations.

### Recommendations

1. **Short-term: Continue using YouTube as a video hosting utility.** No need to invest in channel building until content production capacity increases.
2. **Medium-term: Consider a "Clinical Precision Investing" video series.** Monthly 10-15 minute GP-hosted conversations about specific clinical domains and the investment landscape within them. This leverages the grand rounds format that physicians are comfortable with.
3. **Optimize existing videos:** Add proper titles, descriptions, and tags so that the Paul Slosar overview video is discoverable by people searching for "physician-led venture capital" or "physician VC fund."
4. **Event content capture:** Record and publish (with speaker permission) selected talks from Women's Health Nexus and similar events. This extends event ROI and provides high-quality content for the Resources page.

## Platform Absence Assessment

### Instagram, Facebook, TikTok: Appropriately Absent

PhyCap's two primary audiences (accredited investor physicians and healthcare technology founders) do not discover or evaluate venture capital funds through Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok. The decision to not maintain these platforms is correct and should not change.

The only exception to monitor: if PhyCap launches a consumer-facing brand initiative (unlikely given its B2B focus) or if a specific event series develops a visual brand that benefits from Instagram documentation (possible for Women's Health Nexus). Even then, this would be a secondary use case, not a primary channel.
