# Website Audit

## Site Structure Overview

The PhyCap website serves as the primary digital front door for both physician LP prospects and healthcare founders. The site communicates the fund's thesis, team, community, and content, but several structural issues limit conversion effectiveness.

### Navigation Architecture

**Primary Navigation Items:**
- Home
- About / Thesis
- Team
- Community
- Resources
- LEARN
- INVEST
- PITCH
- Contact

**Critical Issue: LEARN, INVEST, and PITCH pages are not publicly accessible.** These three pages represent the highest-intent actions for both primary audience segments (physician LPs wanting to learn and invest, healthcare founders wanting to pitch). Their inaccessibility means that the website captures attention but cannot convert it. Every prospect who clicks these links and receives no content is a lost conversion opportunity.

---

## Page-by-Page Assessment

### Homepage

**What Works:**
- Rich and detailed content that communicates the fund's thesis with specificity. The homepage does not read like a generic VC landing page; it reads like a clinical investment thesis.
- Video embed of Paul Slosar overview provides a human introduction to the fund. Video is an appropriate format for a physician audience accustomed to grand rounds and CME presentations.
- The clinical precision positioning is evident: language references workflow optimization, SaMD, and women's health rather than vague "healthcare innovation" terminology.
- The 168+ years of combined clinical experience and 13+ specialties are strong credibility anchors when surfaced early.

**What Does Not Work:**
- Multiple competing CTAs on the homepage dilute user intent. A physician LP and a healthcare founder have fundamentally different next steps, but the homepage does not clearly route them.
- No "As Seen In" or media mention section. For a Fund I building credibility, third-party validation (media coverage, event recognition, partnership logos) would strengthen the authority signal.
- No portfolio showcase. Even with only BioReact publicly confirmed, a portfolio section with thesis-aligned context would demonstrate active deployment.

**Recommendations:**
- Add two clear pathway CTAs: "For Physicians" and "For Founders" that route visitors to audience-specific landing pages
- Add a media/recognition bar (Women's Health Nexus event, any press coverage, partnership logos)
- Add a portfolio section, even if it currently features only BioReact with a "Portfolio companies announced as they close" message

---

### Team Page

**What Works:**
- All 10 GPs are listed with their specialties, which is the most important information for both audience segments
- The depth of the team (13+ specialties, advanced degrees) is communicated

**What Does Not Work:**
- Accordion format requires visitors to click-to-expand each GP bio individually. A physician evaluating whether the team includes someone from their specialty must click through up to 10 accordions. This creates friction, especially on mobile.
- No grouping by specialty or clinical domain. A women's health founder looking for the OB-GYN GP must scan all 10 names.
- No indication of which GPs are most active in deal sourcing, LP relationships, or specific clinical domains
- Co-founder Dutch Rojas and non-physician team members (Jerald Dawkins PhD, Alex Tang PA-C MBA) are listed alongside physician GPs without clear role differentiation

**Recommendations:**
- Replace accordion format with expanded bio cards that show specialty, credentials, and a one-sentence clinical focus without requiring a click
- Add a "Clinical Expertise Map" visual that shows which specialties are covered across the GP team
- Consider grouping by clinical domain or thesis area (workflow optimization, SaMD, women's health) to help founders identify relevant GPs
- Add brief indicators of each GP's fund role (deal sourcing, LP relations, portfolio support)

---

### Community Page

**What Works:**
- Four LP testimonials (Brian Russell MD, Emil Engels MD, Maxwell Ginsberg MD, James Newman MD) provide social proof from credible physician voices
- The testimonial language is strong and specific: "a seat at the table," "play an active role in shaping the future of medicine," "collaborating with colleagues who bring sharp insights"

**What Does Not Work:**
- Testimonials are brief pull quotes. They establish tone but do not answer the operational questions a prospective LP has (what does membership look like, what events exist, how does clinical engagement work)
- No description of Innovation Circle programming, cadence, or membership benefits
- No visual or narrative representation of the community (event photos from Women's Health Nexus, member count, specialty distribution)
- The page does not differentiate between the community experience and the investment commitment; a visitor cannot tell whether community membership requires LP status

**Recommendations:**
- Expand testimonials into short narrative formats (3-5 sentences each) that describe the LP experience, not just the motivation
- Add an Innovation Circle overview section: what it is, what members do, how it connects to the fund
- Add event showcase: Women's Health Nexus photos, attendance numbers (200+), venue (Congressional Country Club), and upcoming events
- Clarify the relationship between community membership and LP commitment

---

### Resources Page

**What Works:**
- 8+ articles covering healthcare trends, physician investing education, technology adoption, healthcare economics, women's health, and democratization of VC
- Content pillars are well-chosen and thesis-aligned
- Clean URL structure and proper heading hierarchy suggest thoughtful content architecture

**What Does Not Work:**
- No content categorization or filtering by topic, audience, or format
- No indication of publication cadence or freshness (are these articles from 2024 or 2025?)
- No author attribution visible (are these written by GPs, or by a content team?)
- No newsletter or content subscription mechanism to capture return visitors
- Articles do not appear to be optimized for search engine discovery (no visible meta descriptions, keyword targeting, or internal linking strategy)

**Recommendations:**
- Add topic filters (Healthcare Economics, Physician Investing, Women's Health, Technology, SaMD/Regulatory)
- Add author attribution with GP bylines where applicable (GP-authored content is significantly more credible than anonymous fund content)
- Add publication dates to signal freshness and ongoing commitment to content
- Add email newsletter signup to capture prospects who are in the Awareness or Consideration stage but not ready to commit
- Implement internal linking between related articles to increase time-on-site and depth of engagement

---

### Contact

**What Works:**
- Contact email (info@46.capital) is provided, establishing a communication channel

**What Does Not Work:**
- A single general inbox for all inquiries (LP prospects, founder pitches, media, partnerships) creates routing challenges and response time uncertainty
- The info@46.capital domain does not match the PhyCap brand name, which may create confusion about organizational identity and the FortySix Venture Capital relationship
- No contact form, which means no structured data collection from inbound inquiries
- No indication of expected response time

**Recommendations:**
- Add a structured contact form with audience-type selector (Physician Investor, Healthcare Founder, Media, Other) to route inquiries appropriately
- Add expected response time commitment ("We respond within 2 business days")
- Consider audience-specific email addresses or intake forms (invest@phycap.com, pitch@phycap.com) as LEARN/INVEST/PITCH pages are built out
- Clarify the 46.capital domain relationship to PhyCap for visitors encountering it for the first time

## Cross-Site Issues

### CTA Architecture

The site currently presents multiple competing calls-to-action without a clear hierarchy:
- LEARN, INVEST, PITCH navigation items (inaccessible)
- Contact email
- Video embed
- Resources articles
- Community testimonials

A visitor who arrives with intent to learn about investing is offered content, community proof, and a video, but not a clear path to "Learn More About Investing." The highest-intent CTAs (LEARN, INVEST, PITCH) are broken, and the fallback is a general email inbox.

**Recommendation:** Establish a two-path CTA architecture:
1. **Physician LP path:** Awareness content leads to "Learn About Physician-Led Investing" leads to "Begin the Conversation" (with a GP matching mechanism)
2. **Founder path:** Thesis alignment content leads to "See If Your Company Fits" leads to "Submit Your Pitch" (structured intake form)

### Mobile Experience

- Accordion format on Team page is especially problematic on mobile
- Multiple navigation items may create a cluttered mobile menu
- Video embed responsiveness should be verified

## Priority Recommendations

### Immediate (Next 30 Days)
1. Make LEARN, INVEST, and PITCH pages accessible with at minimum placeholder content and a clear next step (contact form, meeting scheduler, or structured intake)
2. Add a portfolio section to the homepage or create a dedicated portfolio page featuring BioReact with thesis context
3. Add publication dates and author attribution to Resources articles

### Short-Term (Next 90 Days)
4. Replace Team page accordion format with expanded bio cards
5. Add two-path CTA architecture (Physician LP path, Founder path)
6. Add newsletter signup mechanism
7. Add contact form with audience-type routing
8. Add event showcase section (Women's Health Nexus, upcoming events)

### Medium-Term (Next 6 Months)
9. Build dedicated landing pages for each audience segment
10. Implement content categorization and filtering on Resources page
11. Add media/recognition bar to homepage
12. Develop SEO-optimized content strategy (see seo-baseline.md)

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No analytics data available.** Without access to Google Analytics, Search Console, or similar tools, traffic volume, bounce rates, time-on-page, and conversion paths are unknown. All assessments are based on structural observation, not performance data.
2. **No mobile testing conducted.** The audit is based on desktop observation. Mobile responsiveness, load times, and interaction patterns on mobile devices are not assessed.
3. **No accessibility audit conducted.** WCAG compliance, screen reader compatibility, and color contrast ratios are not evaluated. Healthcare audiences may include physicians with accessibility needs.
4. **Backend/CMS unknown.** The technology stack, hosting, CMS platform, and analytics implementation are not visible from the front end. These affect the feasibility and timeline of recommended changes.
5. **LEARN, INVEST, and PITCH page intended content unknown.** These pages may have been built and then made private, or they may never have been built. The intended content for each would inform the priority and scope of recommendations.
6. **No competitive site benchmarking.** How does the PhyCap site compare structurally to Scrub Capital, LRVHealth, or Rock Health? A comparative analysis would reveal industry expectations for healthcare VC websites.
