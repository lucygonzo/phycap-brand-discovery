# Content Strategy

## Current State Assessment

### Existing Content Pillars (from Resources Page)

PhyCap's Resources page contains 8+ articles organized around the following thematic areas:

1. **Healthcare Trends:** Broad coverage of healthcare industry developments
2. **Physician Investing Education:** Content designed to help physicians understand venture capital mechanics
3. **Technology Adoption:** Coverage of digital health, AI, wearables, and clinical technology
4. **Healthcare Economics:** Analysis of healthcare system sustainability, cost structures, and market dynamics
5. **Women's Health:** Content related to maternal health, women's health technology, and health equity
6. **Democratization of VC:** Framing of physician-led investing as a structural shift in capital allocation

### The Ownership Gap

PhyCap's content currently splits between two categories: topics the fund owns through direct expertise and lived experience, and topics the fund covers because they are trending in healthcare.

**Topics PhyCap Owns:**
- Physician-led venture capital (no one else has 10 GPs across 13 specialties evaluating deals through clinical lenses)
- Clinical precision in investment evaluation (the methodology of applying clinical diagnostic thinking to deal assessment)
- Physician workflow optimization (the GPs know what tools fail at 2 AM during a call shift because they have used those tools at 2 AM during a call shift)
- SaMD from the clinician's perspective (not the regulatory consultant's perspective or the developer's perspective, but the end-user physician's perspective)
- Women's health investment (Robin Noble MD as a practicing OB-GYN GP gives this thesis area lived clinical authority)
- The structural exclusion of physicians from healthcare capital decisions (the emotional and professional core of the LP value proposition)

**Topics PhyCap Chases:**
- AI in healthcare (everyone covers this; PhyCap does not have a differentiated AI take unless it is filtered through specific clinical specialty applications)
- Wearables and remote monitoring (interesting but outside PhyCap's core thesis unless tied to clinical workflow integration)
- Broad digital health trends (what Rock Health, CB Insights, and every healthcare VC newsletter already covers)
- General VC landscape (fundraising climate, macroeconomic trends, deal activity data)

**The Strategic Problem:** When PhyCap publishes content about broad AI trends or general digital health market dynamics, it competes with Rock Health, a16z Bio, and STAT News. PhyCap will lose that competition because those organizations have larger audiences, more data, and dedicated research teams. When PhyCap publishes content about how a radiologist evaluates an AI diagnostic tool differently than a VC associate with a finance degree, it has no competition. Zero. That content does not exist anywhere else.

## Recommended Content Pillar Framework

### Pillar 1: Clinical Precision Investing (Owned)

**What This Covers:**
- How physician clinical judgment applies to venture deal evaluation
- Specific examples of clinical expertise changing investment decisions (anonymized if needed)
- The methodology: what does "clinical due diligence" look like when the diligence team includes the end-user physician?
- Comparisons between how a physician evaluates a clinical workflow tool versus how a traditional VC evaluates it

**Why It Matters:** This is PhyCap's most differentiated content territory. No other VC fund can produce this content with the same credibility because no other fund has 10 GPs across 13 specialties who actively practice medicine.

**Content Formats:**
- GP-authored LinkedIn posts (first-person narrative): "I evaluated a clinical decision support tool last month. Here is what I saw that a non-clinician investor would have missed."
- Long-form articles for Resources page (800-1,200 words)
- Short video (3-5 minutes): GP explaining their clinical evaluation process for a specific technology category

**Cadence:** 2x per month across channels

---

### Pillar 2: The Physician's Stake (Owned)

**What This Covers:**
- Why physicians should participate in healthcare capital allocation, not just healthcare delivery
- The structural problem: who currently makes the investment decisions that determine which clinical tools get built?
- Physician investing education: fund mechanics, alternative investment basics, how VC differs from real estate or public equity
- LP stories and testimonials that connect personal clinical frustration to investment motivation

**Why It Matters:** This pillar feeds the LP pipeline directly. The testimonial language ("a seat at the table," "play an active role in shaping the future of medicine") is the emotional center of the LP conversion path. Content in this pillar validates the prospective LP's frustration and presents PhyCap as the response.

**Content Formats:**
- LinkedIn posts featuring LP testimonial language with GP commentary
- "Physician Investor Basics" article series for the Resources page (designed to replace the missing LEARN page content)
- Event recap content from Women's Health Nexus and similar gatherings that showcase the community

**Cadence:** 2x per month across channels

---

### Pillar 3: Specialty Deep Dives (Owned, Underleveraged)

**What This Covers:**
- Clinical specialty-specific analysis of technology adoption, workflow challenges, and investment opportunities
- Each GP writes from their specialty: Robin Noble on OB-GYN workflow and women's health tech, Giovanni Lorenz on radiology AI, Vipul Kella on emergency department technology, Tim Martens on pediatric cardiothoracic innovation, Fred Liss on orthopedic/hand surgery tools
- This is where "13 specialties" becomes a content production engine, not just a team slide statistic

**Why It Matters:** Specialty deep dives serve three purposes simultaneously:
1. They attract physician prospects in each specialty (LP pipeline)
2. They signal clinical depth to founders building in those domains (founder pipeline)
3. They differentiate PhyCap from every healthcare VC that publishes generic "state of digital health" content

**Content Formats:**
- GP-authored LinkedIn posts from the individual GP's profile (not the company page)
- Quarterly specialty reports for the Resources page (1,500-2,000 words)
- Panel discussions or conversations between GPs at events

**Cadence:** 1 specialty deep dive per month, rotating across GPs

---

### Pillar 4: Women's Health and Health Equity (Owned, High Priority)

**What This Covers:**
- The investment case for women's health technology (market sizing, clinical need, structural underfunding)
- Women's Health Nexus event content, recaps, and speaker highlights
- Robin Noble's clinical perspective on OB-GYN innovation gaps
- FemTech founder spotlights (as portfolio companies are confirmed)
- Health equity framing: how clinical workflow optimization in underserved settings creates both clinical impact and market opportunity

**Why It Matters:** Women's health is PhyCap's highest-signal thesis area for category ownership. The combination of Robin Noble's clinical authority, the Women's Health Nexus event (200+ attendees, Congressional Country Club), and the growing FemTech VC market ($2-3B) creates a content position that no competitor currently occupies with the same clinical credibility.

**Content Formats:**
- Women's Health Nexus event content (pre-event promotion, live updates, post-event recaps and key insights)
- Robin Noble-authored articles and LinkedIn posts on women's health clinical innovation
- Data-driven content on women's health funding gaps (partner with Rock Health or similar for data)
- Founder spotlight interviews with women's health entrepreneurs

**Cadence:** 2x per month (increasing around event periods)

---

### Pillar 5: SaMD and Regulatory Intelligence (Owned, Niche)

**What This Covers:**
- FDA SaMD landscape from a clinician-investor perspective
- How PhyCap evaluates SaMD regulatory risk differently than a traditional VC (because the GPs understand clinical risk tolerance)
- Class I versus Class II distinction explained for physician investors
- SaMD founder guidance: what PhyCap looks for in a regulatory strategy

**Why It Matters:** This is a niche content area, but a high-value one. SaMD founders are actively seeking investors who understand the regulatory landscape. Content that demonstrates regulatory fluency attracts exactly the right founders to PhyCap's pipeline.

**Content Formats:**
- Articles for Resources page (800-1,200 words)
- LinkedIn posts connecting specific FDA decisions or guidance documents to PhyCap's thesis
- Occasional cross-pillar content with specialty deep dives (e.g., "SaMD in Radiology: What a Radiologist Investor Looks For")

**Cadence:** 1x per month

## GP Content Activation Plan

### The Problem

PhyCap has 10 GPs across 13 specialties, but the content output is concentrated in 2 voices (Paul Slosar and Dutch Rojas). This means 80% of the fund's clinical content capacity is dormant.

### The Solution: Tiered GP Content Commitment

**Tier 1 (High Activity): Paul Slosar, Dutch Rojas**
- Continue current posting cadence (2-4x per week)
- Serve as content models for other GPs
- Host or lead video content and event presentations

**Tier 2 (Monthly Contributors): Robin Noble, Vipul Kella, Giovanni Lorenz, Joseph Jasser**
- One substantive LinkedIn post per month from their clinical domain
- One quote or commentary contribution to a company page post per month
- Engagement (likes/comments) on company page posts 2-3x per week

**Tier 3 (Quarterly Contributors): Tim Martens, Fred Liss, Jerald Dawkins, Alex Tang**
- One substantive LinkedIn post per quarter
- One specialty deep dive article contribution per year
- Engagement (likes/comments) on company page posts 1-2x per week

### Content Support for GPs

To activate dormant GP voices, PhyCap should provide:
1. **Monthly topic prompts** aligned to the content calendar (e.g., "Robin: this month's theme is cervical screening access. Here are three angles you could write about.")
2. **Draft-to-publish support:** A content team member interviews the GP for 15 minutes, drafts the post, and the GP reviews and publishes from their personal profile.
3. **Performance feedback:** Monthly summary showing each GP which of their posts performed best and why, building their confidence and content instincts over time.

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No content performance data exists.** Which existing articles on the Resources page perform best? Which LinkedIn posts generate the most engagement? Without this data, the content strategy is based on thesis alignment and audience analysis, not proven content performance.
2. **GP content activation plan is untested.** The tiered commitment model assumes GP willingness and capacity. Some GPs may be unable or unwilling to contribute content, even at the quarterly tier. A GP content survey would reveal actual capacity.
3. **No content production workflow defined.** Who writes, edits, designs, and publishes content? Is there a dedicated content resource, or does this fall to an already-stretched team? The strategy requires production capacity to execute.
4. **No paid amplification budget or strategy.** Organic content alone will not grow LinkedIn followers from 1,132 to the levels needed for Fund II awareness. A paid content amplification strategy and budget should complement the organic plan.
5. **No content repurposing workflow.** A single GP interview could produce a LinkedIn post, a Resources article, a video clip, and an email newsletter excerpt. This multiplicative approach is not currently structured.
6. **Newsletter strategy not developed.** Email is the highest-converting content channel for financial products. PhyCap has no email newsletter, which means all content distribution depends on social media algorithms.
7. **Event content capture not systematized.** Women's Health Nexus produced 200+ attendees and presumably multiple presentations, panels, and conversations. Whether this content was captured for ongoing distribution is unknown.
