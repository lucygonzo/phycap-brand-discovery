# Funnel Audits

## Funnel 1: Physician LP Path

### Stage Map

```
AWARENESS > COMMUNITY > QUALIFICATION > INVESTMENT
```

### Stage-by-Stage Audit

#### Stage 1: Awareness
**How physicians discover PhyCap**: GP personal LinkedIn posts, company LinkedIn page (1,132 followers), podcast appearances (Becker's, The Imaging Wire, Simple Tech Talk), RyOrtho press coverage, Women's Health Nexus event, peer referrals from existing LPs, webinar series.

**What works**: Content quality is strong. Podcast appearances on credible outlets. The Women's Health Nexus event created significant awareness within the women's health and policy communities. LP testimonials on the Community page provide social proof.

**Where it breaks**: SEO presence for competitive search terms ("physician venture capital," "physician investor," "healthcare VC for doctors") is likely minimal. No paid acquisition channels are visible. No "As Seen In" media section consolidates press coverage. The company LinkedIn page has moderate reach (1,132 followers). GP personal brands are the primary awareness driver, but they are not yet operating at full cadence.

**Leak severity**: Moderate. Awareness is growing through organic channels but is limited by GP posting consistency and the absence of SEO-driven inbound.

---

#### Stage 2: Community
**How physicians engage after awareness**: Newsletter signup (homepage and Community page forms), Innovation Circle membership, webinar attendance, Regional meetup attendance, Resources page content consumption.

**What works**: Multiple email capture points across the site. Medical Specialty field in signup form enables segmentation. Tiered community model (free community vs. investor members) creates a natural progression path. Webinar series (with named speakers Paul Slosar, Tracy Poole, Vipul Kella, Joseph Jasser) provides regular engagement touchpoints. Content on the Resources page covers relevant topics.

**Where it breaks**: The LEARN page was not publicly accessible during research. If this is an education hub for prospective LPs, its inaccessibility removes a critical engagement tool. No visible post-signup nurture sequence is documented. A physician who signs up for the newsletter has no clear next step communicated. The "Fund Overview" is mentioned on the Community page but the link was not accessible during research. The transition from "interested community member" to "qualified LP prospect" has no visible mechanism.

**Leak severity**: High. The community layer captures interest but has no documented conversion mechanism to move physicians from "interested" to "qualified."

---

#### Stage 3: Qualification
**How physicians move from community to LP consideration**: 1:1 call booking ("Book a 1:1 Call" CTA on Community page, "Schedule time with us" in header), presumed email outreach, event follow-up conversations.

**What works**: The "Schedule time with us" CTA appears on every page. The 1:1 call model is appropriate for high-touch LP recruitment. Webinars likely serve as qualification events (attendees who ask questions or stay for Q&A signal higher intent).

**Where it breaks**: Multiple CTAs compete on the Community page ("JOIN NOW," "Book a 1:1 Call," "Schedule time with us," "INVEST WITH US"). A physician who is ready to explore investing faces choice paralysis rather than a clear next step. No accredited investor verification is visible on the website, despite being required for VC fundraising. No FAQ or "What to Expect" content addresses common LP questions (minimum investment, time commitment, legal structure, tax implications).

**Leak severity**: High. The moment of highest intent has the most friction.

---

#### Stage 4: Investment
**How physicians commit capital**: The INVEST page (linked in navigation and footer as "Invest with us") was not publicly accessible during research. The presumed path is: 1:1 call with GP > accredited investor verification > legal documentation > capital commitment.

**What works**: The pathway likely works well in practice (PhyCap is actively raising, which means LPs are converting). The personal touch of GP-led conversations is appropriate for physician audience expectations.

**Where it breaks**: The INVEST page being inaccessible during research is the single largest funnel failure. A physician who has read the content, attended a webinar, and decided to explore investing clicks "INVEST WITH US" and gets a "Could not find this page" error. Even if the page is intentionally gated (requiring authentication or a direct link), the error message does not communicate that. It communicates that the page is broken. This is a revenue-impacting issue.

**Leak severity**: Critical. The conversion endpoint is not publicly reachable.

## Funnel 2: Healthcare Founder Path

### Stage Map

```
DISCOVERY > PITCH SUBMISSION > DILIGENCE > INVESTMENT
```

### Stage-by-Stage Audit

#### Stage 1: Discovery
**How founders find PhyCap**: LinkedIn content, GP network referrals, Women's Health Nexus event, Resources page articles, podcast mentions, PitchBook listing, search ("healthcare VC" terms).

**What works**: The investment thesis is clearly stated on the homepage and Priorities page. Three focus areas (Workflow Optimization, SaMD, Women's Health/Health Inequity) are specific enough for founders to self-qualify. The value proposition to founders is articulated: "Credibility and industry influence, strategic partnerships, demand-driven innovation, built-in pilot users and customer base."

**Where it breaks**: No dedicated founder-facing landing page exists. The founder value proposition is buried within the general homepage content rather than presented on a standalone page designed for founders. The 500+ deals screened stat suggests strong inbound, but founders who arrive at the website have to navigate a site primarily designed for LP recruitment to find founder-relevant information.

**Leak severity**: Moderate. Founders can find the information but the site does not prioritize their journey.

---

#### Stage 2: Pitch Submission
**How founders submit**: The PITCH page is linked in the primary navigation.

**What works**: The navigation placement is prominent. Founders who find PhyCap know immediately that there is a pitch path.

**Where it breaks**: The PITCH page was not publicly accessible during research. A founder who arrives at the website, self-qualifies against the investment thesis, and clicks "PITCH" gets a dead end. No alternative submission path is visible (no email address for pitches, no Typeform or application link, no "How to Pitch" guide). The contact page exists but uses a generic form with no pitch-specific fields.

**Leak severity**: Critical. The primary founder conversion mechanism is not publicly reachable.

---

#### Stage 3: Diligence
**How PhyCap evaluates deals**: Not publicly documented. The tagline ("Investing with Clinical Precision") implies a rigorous process, and the GP roster suggests multi-specialty clinical review, but no public-facing content explains what the diligence process actually involves.

**What works**: The underlying process is likely strong (the GP roster's clinical breadth enables a level of diligence no generalist VC can match). 500+ deals screened annually indicates a functioning evaluation pipeline.

**Where it breaks**: The process is invisible. Founders have no way to understand what PhyCap's diligence looks like, how long it takes, or what criteria drive decisions beyond the three stated investment priorities. For founders evaluating multiple VC options, the opacity of PhyCap's process is a competitive disadvantage. Founders prefer investors who are transparent about their process.

**Leak severity**: Moderate. This is a content gap, not a structural failure. Creating a "How We Evaluate" page or section would resolve it.

---

#### Stage 4: Investment
**How PhyCap makes investments**: Standard VC investment process (term sheet, legal documentation, capital deployment). Not publicly documented.

**What works**: BioReact is a publicly confirmed investment, demonstrating that the pipeline produces outcomes.

**Where it breaks**: No portfolio company logos, case studies, or founder testimonials appear on the website. A founder evaluating PhyCap cannot see evidence of successful past investments beyond a single PitchBook listing. The "What happens after PhyCap invests" narrative (clinical adoption support, pilot testing, strategic partnerships) is described in value proposition language but not demonstrated with examples.

**Leak severity**: Moderate. Improves naturally as the portfolio grows and case studies are developed.

## Summary

| Funnel | Stage | Leak Severity |
|--------|-------|--------------|
| **Physician LP** | Awareness | Moderate |
| | Community | High |
| | Qualification | High |
| | Investment | Critical |
| **Founder** | Discovery | Moderate |
| | Pitch Submission | Critical |
| | Diligence | Moderate |
| | Investment | Moderate |

**Highest-priority fixes**:
1. Resolve INVEST and PITCH page accessibility (both funnels' critical breaks).
2. Build LP qualification mechanism (post-signup nurture, FAQ, "What to Expect" content).
3. Clarify CTA hierarchy per audience per page (reduce choice paralysis at qualification stage).
4. Create founder-facing content explaining the diligence process.
