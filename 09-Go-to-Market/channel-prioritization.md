# Channel Prioritization

## Prioritization Framework

Channels are ranked by four criteria:
1. **Audience alignment:** Does the channel reach physician LPs and healthcare founders?
2. **Cost efficiency:** Can the channel produce results at Fund I budget levels?
3. **GP leverage:** Does the channel multiply GP effort rather than requiring dedicated marketing staff?
4. **Compounding returns:** Does investment in the channel build durable assets (audience, content library, email list)?

---

## Channel Rankings

### Tier 1: Primary Investment

#### 1. LinkedIn (Company Page + GP Personal Brands)
**Investment level:** High (primary channel, highest time allocation)

**Why primary:**
- Physician decision-makers (both LP prospects and founders) use LinkedIn as their primary professional platform
- 10 GPs across 13+ specialties represent 10 potential content distribution nodes, each with their own professional network
- The company page (1,132 followers) serves as the brand hub, but GP personal posts drive reach and engagement
- Content creation cost is low (GP expertise is the raw material), distribution cost is zero
- Every post builds a searchable content archive that compounds over time

**Execution model:**
- Company page: 3-4 posts per week (thesis updates, portfolio news, event announcements, curated industry commentary)
- GP personal brands: Each active GP posts 2-4 times per month using templates and content frameworks from Brand HQ
- Content mix: 40% clinical insight (what physicians see that investors miss), 30% thesis commentary (SaMD trends, Women's Health, workflow optimization), 20% fund and portfolio updates, 10% personal/professional narrative

**Metrics to track:**
- Company page followers (baseline: 1,132, target: 2,500 by Q3 2026)
- GP post engagement rates (likes, comments, shares)
- Inbound inquiries attributed to LinkedIn (LP interest, founder outreach)
- Profile views for GP accounts

**Key risk:** GP content consistency. If GPs do not post regularly, the channel underperforms. Playbooks and templates mitigate this but do not eliminate it.

### Tier 2: Strategic Investment

#### 2. Events (Women's Health Nexus Model + Webinars)
**Investment level:** Medium-High (significant per-event cost, but high per-interaction value)

**Why Tier 2:**
- Women's Health Nexus proved the model: 200+ attendees at Congressional Country Club created LP relationships, deal flow, and brand visibility that no digital channel can replicate
- In-person events convert physician prospects at higher rates than digital touchpoints because physicians value peer relationships and in-person credibility signals
- Webinars provide a lower-cost, higher-frequency complement to anchor events

**Execution model:**
- Anchor event: One major event per year (Women's Health Nexus or thesis-aligned equivalent), targeting 200-300 attendees
- Webinar series: Quarterly webinars (60-90 minutes) on thesis-aligned topics, featuring GP expertise and portfolio company participation
- Regional touchpoints: Smaller gatherings (dinners, roundtables) in markets with high physician LP density

**Metrics to track:**
- Event attendance and attendee quality (physician specialties, investment capacity)
- Post-event LP conversion (attendee to prospect to commitment)
- Webinar registration and attendance rates
- Event-sourced deal flow

**Key risk:** Event economics. A 200+ person event at a venue like Congressional Country Club requires meaningful budget. ROI must be tracked rigorously to justify continued investment.

#### 3. Substack / Long-Form Content Platform
**Investment level:** Medium (requires consistent writing effort but minimal direct cost)

**Why Tier 2:**
- Long-form content (1,000-2,500 words) builds thesis authority in ways that LinkedIn posts cannot
- Substack or an equivalent newsletter platform builds a direct email relationship with subscribers, independent of algorithm changes
- Content can be repurposed: a single long-form piece generates multiple LinkedIn posts, webinar topics, and LP conversation starters
- Rock Health's dominance in healthcare VC thought leadership shows that content-driven brand building works in this category

**Execution model:**
- Frequency: 2 articles per month (bi-weekly)
- Content sources: GP clinical perspectives on thesis areas, market analysis, Women's Health investment landscape, SaMD regulatory updates
- Format: Mix of GP-bylined pieces (leveraging clinical authority) and fund-level analysis
- Distribution: Cross-posted to LinkedIn, included in email nurture sequences, referenced in LP materials

**Metrics to track:**
- Subscriber count and growth rate
- Open rates and click-through rates
- Content-to-LP-inquiry attribution
- Social sharing and engagement on cross-posted content

**Key risk:** Writing capacity. Long-form content requires either a dedicated writer who understands healthcare VC or GPs willing to invest meaningful time in writing. Ghost-writing with GP review is the likely operational model.

### Tier 3: Opportunistic Investment

#### 4. Podcast Appearances (Earned Media)
**Investment level:** Low (time cost for GP appearances, no production cost)

**Why Tier 3:**
- Healthcare and VC podcast ecosystem is large and growing. GP appearances on relevant shows extend PhyCap's reach to audiences already engaged with healthcare innovation and physician investing
- Earned media (appearing as a guest) has higher credibility than owned media (publishing your own content)
- Each appearance creates a durable content asset (episode recording) that can be referenced and shared

**Execution model:**
- Target 1-2 podcast appearances per quarter across GPs
- Prioritize shows focused on: healthcare innovation, physician finance/investing, Women's Health, SaMD/digital health, VC industry
- Develop GP media prep (talking points aligned with thesis, brand-consistent messaging)
- Track and promote appearances across LinkedIn and other channels

**Metrics to track:**
- Number of appearances per quarter
- Audience reach of target podcasts
- Inbound inquiries attributed to podcast appearances
- Social engagement on appearance promotion posts

**Key risk:** Low control over timing and narrative. Podcast hosts set the agenda. GP media prep mitigates but does not eliminate messaging inconsistency.

#### 5. Email Nurture
**Investment level:** Low (infrastructure cost for email platform, minimal ongoing cost)

**Why Tier 3:**
- Email nurture converts warm leads (event attendees, webinar registrants, LinkedIn connections who express interest) into LP commitments over time
- Physician LP decision cycles are long (months to years). Consistent, non-aggressive email touchpoints keep PhyCap visible during that decision process
- Email is the only channel where PhyCap owns the audience relationship entirely (no algorithm, no platform risk)

**Execution model:**
- Build segmented email lists: LP prospects, founders, Innovation Circle members, event attendees
- Monthly newsletter (digest of LinkedIn content, Substack highlights, fund updates)
- Event-triggered sequences (post-event follow-up, post-webinar nurture)
- LP-specific sequences for prospects in active evaluation

**Metrics to track:**
- List size by segment
- Open rates and click-through rates by segment
- Email-to-meeting conversion for LP prospects
- Unsubscribe rates (signal of content quality and frequency calibration)

**Key risk:** Email alone does not generate leads. It converts and nurtures leads generated by other channels. If LinkedIn, events, and content do not feed the email list, this channel has no audience.

## Channel Interaction Model

```
LinkedIn (GP Posts) --> Drives awareness and inbound interest
        |
        v
Events / Webinars --> Converts interest into relationships
        |
        v
Substack / Content --> Deepens engagement and builds authority
        |
        v
Email Nurture --> Maintains relationship through LP decision cycle
        |
        v
LP Commitment / Founder Deal Flow

Podcast Appearances --> Feed back into LinkedIn reach and content library
```

Each channel reinforces the others. LinkedIn drives event registrations. Events generate email list additions. Substack content fuels LinkedIn posts. Podcast appearances create LinkedIn content moments. Email nurture keeps all touchpoints connected across a long decision timeline.

## Budget Allocation Guidance

| Channel | Recommended Budget Share | Primary Cost Driver |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------|
| LinkedIn | 10-15% | Content tools, design templates, occasional promotion |
| Events | 40-50% | Venue, production, attendee experience, travel |
| Substack/Content | 10-15% | Writing support, editing, design |
| Podcast | 5% | Media prep, promotional assets |
| Email | 10-15% | Email platform, list management |
| Reserve | 10-15% | Opportunistic spending on emerging channels |

Note: Events absorb the largest budget share because in-person physician engagement has the highest conversion value. Digital channels are lower cost but require consistent time investment from the GP team.

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No baseline metrics for most channels.** Beyond LinkedIn followers (1,132), current performance data for other channels is not available because most channels are not yet active.
2. **GP content willingness and capacity not surveyed.** The LinkedIn strategy assumes GP participation, but individual readiness assessments have not been completed.
3. **No competitive channel analysis.** How Scrub Capital, Rock Health, and other competitors allocate across channels would inform PhyCap's approach, but this data is not compiled here.
4. **Event ROI from Women's Health Nexus not quantified.** The November 2025 event drew 200+ attendees, but the number of LP conversions, deals sourced, and cost-per-outcome are not documented.
5. **No paid media assessment.** LinkedIn ads, sponsored content, and other paid digital channels are not evaluated here. For a Fund I with a limited marketing budget, paid media may not be appropriate, but the decision should be explicit.
6. **Podcast target list not developed.** The strategy references healthcare and VC podcasts but does not identify specific shows or hosts to target.
