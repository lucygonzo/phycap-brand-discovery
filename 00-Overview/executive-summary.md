# Executive Summary

## Key Takeaway

PhyCap is the only physician-led venture capital fund where both the lead GP and the women's health GP hold Dartmouth's Master of Health Care Delivery Science, a graduate degree in healthcare systems architecture that transforms how they evaluate every deal. That credential, combined with a GP roster spanning the full arc of human medicine from birth through end-of-life care, gives PhyCap a diligence capability no competitor fund can replicate. The brand has not yet told this story.

---

## What We Researched

This Brand HQ was built from a comprehensive Raw Discovery audit (January 2026) covering PhyCap's website, competitive landscape, audience signals, content ecosystem, and digital presence. We supplemented the Raw Discovery with targeted research into the Dartmouth MHCDS program curriculum, physician investor demographics and market sizing, competitor brand systems (with emphasis on Scrub Capital as the closest structural parallel), and SEO keyword positioning for the physician-led VC category.

The research spans 19 vault pages across brand identity, visual and verbal systems, audience strategy, competitive positioning, go-to-market planning, and a PhyCap-specific GP Content Strategy built around four individual physician brands.

## What Surprised Us

1. **The MHCDS credential is almost invisible.** Paul Slosar and Robin Noble both hold Dartmouth's Master of Health Care Delivery Science, a 12-month interdisciplinary program jointly run by the Tuck School of Business and the Geisel School of Medicine. The program trains physicians in systems thinking, data-driven decision making, healthcare informatics, and delivery optimization. Graduates do not just understand clinical medicine; they understand why healthcare systems fail and how to redesign them. No competitor fund has a single GP with this credential, let alone two. PhyCap's website lists the degree after their names but never explains what it means or why it matters. This is the single largest missed opportunity in the current brand.

2. **The LP testimonials reveal something deeper than investment interest.** Brian Russell MD wrote about "a healthcare system that is unsustainable in its current form." Emil Engels MD wrote about having "a seat at the table." Maxwell Ginsberg MD wrote about wanting to "play an active role in shaping the future of medicine." These physicians are not chasing returns. They are seeking repatriation of structural power that the healthcare system has taken from them. PhyCap's messaging currently speaks to investment opportunity. The audience is telling you they want agency.

3. **The Women's Health Nexus was a category-defining move, not a networking event.** A fund less than two years old convened 200+ clinicians, founders, operators, policymakers, and investors at Congressional Country Club in Bethesda, MD, with law firm sponsors (Foley & Lardner, Sweetnam Schuster & Schwartz, Scale LLP) and a media co-host (The American Bazaar). That venue, that audience composition, and those sponsors signal institutional credibility that most Fund I vehicles spend years building. The event has no visible brand system, recap content, or strategic follow-through on the website.

4. **The GP roster covers the full clinical lifecycle, and nobody has said so.** Tim Martens (Pediatric Cardiothoracic Surgery), Robin Noble (OB-GYN), Vipul Kella (Emergency Medicine), Paul Slosar and Fred Liss (Orthopedics/Spine and Hand), Joseph Jasser (Internal Medicine), Giovanni Lorenz (Radiology/Cardiothoracic), and Alex Tang (PA-C). From neonatal cardiac care through reproductive health through acute emergency intervention through musculoskeletal aging through diagnostic imaging. When a portfolio company enters PhyCap's diligence process, every clinical angle has a specialist reviewing the product. No competitor fund has this breadth within a physician-led model.

5. **The contact email is info@46.capital, not @phycapfund.com.** This is more than a domain inconsistency. For a fund building a distinct physician-led brand identity, routing all communication through a co-manager's domain undermines the independence PhyCap needs to own its category.
