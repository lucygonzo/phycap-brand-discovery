# Audience Personas

## Persona 1: The Physician LP

### Dr. Mara Okonkwo, Orthopedic Surgeon
**Age:** 47 | **Location:** Suburban Atlanta | **Specialty:** Orthopedic Surgery (Sports Medicine) | **Practice Setting:** Large Multi-Specialty Group | **Household Income:** ~$650K | **Net Worth:** ~$2.2M | **LP Status:** Prospective

---

#### Day in the Life

5:45 AM. Mara is in her car, listening to a healthcare economics podcast while driving to the surgical center. Today she has three arthroscopic procedures, a new patient clinic in the afternoon, and a department meeting at 4 PM where administration will present the latest EHR workflow changes. Nobody asked the surgeons what they needed before designing the new documentation templates.

Between cases, she checks her phone. LinkedIn shows a post from a physician she met at a conference last year: something about a venture fund run entirely by practicing doctors. She reads it while eating a protein bar in the surgeon's lounge. The post is specific. It names the clinical workflow problem, describes the technology solution in language she actually uses, and links to an article about SaMD regulatory pathways. This is not a financial advisor pitching her another real estate syndication. This is someone who understands what happens between the OR and the post-op note.

She taps through to the PhyCap website. The homepage makes sense to her. She sees the team page and recognizes the specialty mix: orthopedics, OB-GYN, emergency medicine, radiology, pediatric cardiothoracic surgery. These are people who operate and admit patients, not people who left medicine after two years of residency to become "healthcare entrepreneurs." She clicks INVEST to learn more. The page does not load. She bookmarks the site and goes back to clinic.

At the department meeting, administration unveils a new pre-authorization workflow tool they purchased from a vendor. It adds four clicks per order. Mara asks if any physicians were involved in the product development or the purchasing decision. The answer is no. The vendor had a slick demo and a competitive price. She thinks about the LinkedIn post. She thinks about the phrase "a seat at the table."

That evening, after her kids are in bed, Mara sits down with her husband Darnell, a civil engineer, to review their investment portfolio. Their financial advisor has them in a diversified mix of index funds, some real estate, and a few alternatives. Darnell asks what she thought of the VC fund she mentioned last week. She pulls up the PhyCap site again. She wants to show him the team, the thesis, the specifics. She tries the INVEST page again. Still nothing.

"I want to learn more," she tells Darnell, "but there is nowhere to actually learn more on the site." She drafts an email to info@46.capital. She wants to know the minimum commitment, the fund structure, and whether there is a way to engage with the community before committing capital. She wonders if anyone will respond, and whether the response will feel like a sales pitch or a conversation between colleagues.

---

#### What Drives Mara

**Core Motivation:** Mara does not need another investment vehicle. She needs evidence that her clinical experience has value in the rooms where healthcare technology decisions are made. The frustration is not financial. The frustration is structural. She has spent 20 years developing expertise that should inform which products get built and funded, but the capital allocation system treats her as a customer, not a decision-maker.

**Identity Signal:** Being an LP in a physician-led fund is a professional identity statement, not just a portfolio allocation. When Mara tells colleagues that she invests in a fund run by 10 practicing physicians across 13 specialties, that says something about who she is and what she believes medicine should become.

**Trust Threshold:** Mara will not commit $150K or more based on a website and a LinkedIn post. She needs a conversation with a GP who practices in or adjacent to her specialty. She needs to hear how clinical expertise actually shapes deal evaluation, not in theory, but in specific examples. Fred Liss MD (Ortho/Hand) on the GP team would be a natural peer conversation.

**Household Decision:** Darnell supports Mara's investment decisions but needs the mechanics explained in non-clinical language: fund structure, timeline to returns, liquidity constraints, risk profile. The PhyCap value proposition must translate for the non-physician partner.

---

## Persona 2: The Healthcare Founder

### Priya Ramachandran, CEO & Co-Founder
**Age:** 36 | **Location:** Boston | **Company:** NovaCervix (fictional) | **Product:** AI-powered cervical cancer screening SaMD, FDA Class II | **Stage:** Seed, raising $2.5M | **Team Size:** 8 | **Revenue:** Pre-revenue, 2 pilot sites

---

#### Day in the Life

7:00 AM. Priya is on a call with her regulatory consultant, reviewing the FDA 510(k) submission timeline for NovaCervix's AI-assisted cervical screening tool. The device analyzes cervical images at the point of care, flagging high-risk lesions that would otherwise require a separate colposcopy referral. The clinical evidence is strong: 94% sensitivity in their pilot data, compared to 72% for standard Pap-plus-HPV screening in underserved settings. But the FDA pathway is specific, and Priya needs to map her predicate device strategy before the next investor conversation.

9:00 AM. She takes a call with a generalist VC partner who led with, "So help me understand, is this like a lab test?" Priya spends 25 minutes explaining the difference between in vitro diagnostics and Software as a Medical Device. The partner is polite but asks if she has "consumer traction metrics." She does not. She has clinical sensitivity data, pilot site outcomes, and a health equity argument that this tool can reach rural and underserved OB-GYN practices where specialist referrals take 6-8 weeks. The VC thanks her for her time. She knows they will pass.

11:00 AM. Her CTO shares a LinkedIn post from Robin Noble MD, a PhyCap GP who is a practicing OB-GYN. The post discusses exactly the cervical screening access gap that NovaCervix addresses. Robin does not pitch the fund in the post. She describes the clinical problem with the precision of someone who has seen it in her own patient panel. Priya sends the post to her co-founder with the note: "These are the investors I want."

1:00 PM. Priya visits the PhyCap website to look for pitch instructions. She clicks PITCH in the navigation. The page does not load. She goes back to the homepage and finds a contact email: info@46.capital. She wonders whether to send a cold email to a general inbox, or whether there is a more direct path to Robin Noble. She checks LinkedIn. Robin's profile is active but does not have a "pitch here" link. Priya drafts a message to Robin directly, referencing the cervical screening post.

3:00 PM. Board call with her angel investors, two of whom are physicians. One of them mentions PhyCap and the Women's Health Nexus event. "They had 200 people at Congressional Country Club. Robin Noble spoke. You should have been there." Priya adds the next PhyCap event to her radar.

6:00 PM. She reviews her fundraising tracker. Of 22 investor conversations this round, 4 were with healthcare-focused funds, and 18 were with generalist VCs. The generalist conversations take twice as long because she has to educate the investor on the clinical and regulatory landscape before she can pitch the company. She calculates that she has spent approximately 40 hours on investor education that a healthcare-specialized fund would not require.

She looks at the PhyCap GP team page. Ten physicians. Thirteen specialties. Robin Noble, OB-GYN. Vipul Kella, Emergency Medicine (relevant for point-of-care applications). Giovanni Lorenz, Radiology (relevant for AI imaging analysis). Three GPs who could evaluate her product without a tutorial. She sends the LinkedIn message to Robin and copies in her co-founder.

---

#### What Drives Priya

**Core Motivation:** Priya is not looking for the highest-prestige VC logo on her cap table. She is looking for investors who will accelerate her FDA pathway, open doors to OB-GYN practice networks for pilot expansion, and defend her business model to downstream investors who may not understand SaMD economics. A $250K check from PhyCap with active GP involvement is worth more to her than $500K from a generalist fund that will ask her to "pivot to consumer" in six months.

**Credibility Need:** When Priya presents to health system procurement committees, she needs to say, "Our investors include practicing OB-GYNs, radiologists, and emergency medicine physicians who evaluated this product through a clinical lens." That sentence changes the room.

**Regulatory Fluency:** Priya needs investors who understand that FDA Class II clearance is a milestone, not a red flag. She needs board members who will not panic when she explains the timeline for clinical evidence generation.

**Women's Health Alignment:** NovaCervix sits at the intersection of two PhyCap thesis areas: SaMD (FDA Class II) and women's health/health inequity. The fund's stated commitment to these areas is not just alignment; for Priya, it is a signal that she will not have to justify why cervical cancer screening in underserved populations is a venture-scale opportunity.

---

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No real founder interview data.** Priya is constructed from pattern recognition across healthcare SaMD founders, not from interviews with actual PhyCap portfolio founders. BioReact's founder perspective would ground this persona.
2. **Physician LP persona skews surgical.** Mara represents the orthopedic/surgical LP, but the GP team spans radiology, internal medicine, emergency medicine, and pediatric specialties. A second LP persona from a non-surgical specialty would capture different motivations and frustrations.
3. **No persona for the "not yet accredited" physician.** Early-career physicians (residents, fellows, early attendings) who are engaged with PhyCap's content and community but cannot yet commit LP capital. This persona matters for long-term pipeline.
4. **Household decision dynamics are assumed.** The spousal involvement in Mara's story is based on general patterns, not PhyCap-specific data. Actual LP conversion data would reveal whether household decision-making is a meaningful friction point.
5. **Founder persona does not address the FortySix relationship.** How does the co-management with FortySix Venture Capital appear to founders? Is it a credibility signal or a source of confusion about who they are actually working with?
