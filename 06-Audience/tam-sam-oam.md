# Total Addressable, Serviceable Addressable, and Obtainable Addressable Market

## Market 1: Physician LP Opportunity

### TAM (Total Addressable Market)

**Universe: All Accredited Investor Physicians in the United States**

- Approximately 1,000,000+ active physicians in the US (AMA Physician Masterfile)
- Approximately 40-50% qualify as accredited investors (earning $200K+ annually), yielding ~400,000-500,000 accredited investor physicians
- Average physician net worth varies by specialty: orthopedic surgeons average $1.5-2M+, primary care physicians average $700K-$1M, cardiologists and radiologists fall between these ranges
- If the average LP commitment to a physician-led VC fund is $100K-$250K, the total capital pool represented by accredited investor physicians is substantial

**TAM Calculation:**
- 450,000 accredited investor physicians (midpoint estimate)
- At an average potential commitment of $150K (PhyCap's minimum check range)
- TAM = ~$67.5B in theoretically addressable physician LP capital

**Reality Check:** The vast majority of these physicians will never invest in a venture capital fund. Most use traditional wealth management (index funds, real estate, retirement accounts). The TAM is useful as a ceiling, not a forecast.

---

### SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)

**Universe: Accredited Investor Physicians Who Have Demonstrated Interest in Alternative Investments and Healthcare Innovation**

Narrowing criteria:
- Physicians who have invested in any alternative asset (VC, PE, real estate syndication, angel investment): estimated 10-15% of accredited investor physicians = ~45,000-67,500
- Further narrowed to those with demonstrated interest in healthcare innovation (attend health-tech conferences, hold advanced degrees in healthcare management/business, active on LinkedIn discussing healthcare systems): estimated 20-30% of the alternative-investment segment = ~9,000-20,000
- Physicians in specialties represented by PhyCap's GP team (13+ specialties covering most of the physician workforce) do not significantly narrow this, as the specialty coverage is broad

**SAM Calculation:**
- ~15,000 physicians (midpoint) who are accredited, alternative-investment-active, and healthcare-innovation-engaged
- At an average commitment of $200K
- SAM = ~$3B in serviceable physician LP capital

**This is the market that PhyCap's thesis, team, and positioning can credibly reach** through its content, events, GP networks, and physician-executive alumni channels. The key differentiator from broader physician-investor platforms: PhyCap offers thesis-specific, clinically-grounded investment, not a broad "doctors investing" community.

### OAM (Obtainable Addressable Market)

**Universe: Physicians PhyCap Can Realistically Convert to LPs in Fund I and Fund II (Next 3-5 Years)**

Narrowing criteria:
- GP network reach: 10 GPs with personal networks of ~50-200 relevant physician contacts each = ~500-2,000 warm connections
- Event pipeline: Women's Health Nexus drew 200+ attendees; 2-3 events per year at similar scale = ~400-600 net new contacts annually
- LinkedIn content reach: 1,132 company followers + GP personal profile audiences; realistic engagement-to-conversation conversion of ~1-3%
- Peer referral from existing LPs (the highest-converting channel): each LP refers 1-2 qualified prospects over 2 years
- Geographic and specialty reach constraints: Fund I is primarily deploying through existing GP networks, which are strongest in their respective clinical communities

**OAM Calculation:**
- Fund I target: $10M from ~40-65 LPs at $150K-$250K average commitment
- Fund II (projected): $25-50M from ~100-200 LPs
- Near-term obtainable LP base (3-5 years): ~150-250 physician LPs
- At an average commitment of $200K
- OAM = ~$30M-$50M in obtainable physician LP capital (Fund I + Fund II)

**The critical constraint is not market size but conversion infrastructure.** The LEARN and INVEST pages being inaccessible means PhyCap is currently converting LPs through direct GP relationships and events, not through a scalable digital education-to-commitment pipeline.

## Market 2: Healthcare Founder / Deal Flow Opportunity

### TAM (Total Addressable Market)

**Universe: All Early-Stage Healthcare Technology Companies Seeking Venture Capital**

- Digital health VC was approximately $10B annually in 2023-2024 (Rock Health, CB Insights)
- This spans thousands of companies across all digital health subsectors
- SaMD market projected at $50B+ by 2028, with hundreds of startups in the FDA pipeline
- Women's health / FemTech VC growing, estimated at $2-3B market capitalization with increasing annual investment

**TAM Calculation:**
- Approximately 1,500-2,500 healthcare technology startups raise Seed or Series A rounds annually in the US
- At average round sizes of $2-5M
- TAM = ~$5-10B in annual Seed-Series A healthcare technology funding

---

### SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)

**Universe: Seed-Series A Healthcare Technology Companies Building Within PhyCap's Three Thesis Areas**

Narrowing to PhyCap's thesis:
1. **Clinical Care Delivery Workflow Optimization:** Estimated 20-25% of digital health startups address clinical workflow (as opposed to payer, pharma, wellness, or consumer health)
2. **Software as a Medical Device (FDA Class I/II):** Estimated 10-15% of digital health startups are pursuing FDA SaMD pathways (many avoid FDA entirely)
3. **Women's Health / Health Inequity:** Estimated 5-8% of digital health startups are focused on women's health or health equity

These categories overlap (a women's health SaMD company would fit two thesis areas), so the combined addressable segment is not additive.

**SAM Calculation:**
- Approximately 300-500 Seed-Series A companies annually that fit at least one PhyCap thesis area
- At PhyCap's check size of $150K-$500K
- SAM = ~$75M-$250M in annual investable deal flow within thesis

---

### OAM (Obtainable Addressable Market)

**Universe: Deals PhyCap Can Realistically Source, Evaluate, and Close**

Narrowing criteria:
- Fund I deployment: $10M target, deploying ~$6-8M into portfolio companies (reserving for fees, expenses, and follow-on)
- Check size: $150K-$500K per deal
- Expected portfolio: 15-30 companies over Fund I life
- Annual deployment pace: 5-10 new investments per year
- Deal flow sources: GP clinical networks, FortySix Venture Capital co-investment pipeline, Women's Health Nexus event deal flow, inbound from LinkedIn/website, healthcare accelerator relationships

**OAM Calculation:**
- 5-10 new investments annually at $150K-$500K = $1M-$5M deployed per year
- Over Fund I life (3-5 year deployment period): ~$6-8M into 15-30 portfolio companies
- OAM = ~$6-8M in obtainable deal deployment (Fund I)

**The critical constraint on the founder/deal flow side is not deal quality but sourcing infrastructure and GP evaluation bandwidth.** Ten GPs with active clinical practices have limited hours for deal evaluation. The PITCH page being inaccessible also means inbound deal flow is diverted to a general inbox rather than a structured intake process.

| Key Constraint | Conversion infrastructure (website, education pipeline) | GP evaluation bandwidth, sourcing channels |

## Strategic Implications

1. **The LP market is large enough.** PhyCap does not have a TAM problem on the LP side. The challenge is conversion, not addressable market. The fund needs to move physicians from "interested" to "committed" more efficiently.

2. **The founder market requires thesis discipline.** At $150K-$500K checks, PhyCap participates in rounds rather than leading them. Thesis specificity (workflow, SaMD, women's health) is the selection mechanism, and broadening the thesis to capture more deal flow would dilute the clinical precision positioning.

3. **Fund I is a proof point, not a market capture strategy.** The $10M Fund I target represents a fraction of a percent of the obtainable market. Its purpose is to demonstrate that physician-led clinical evaluation produces differentiated returns, which then justifies Fund II at 3-5x the size.

4. **Women's health is the highest-signal thesis area.** The $2-3B FemTech market is growing, underfunded relative to clinical need, and PhyCap has a differentiated position (Robin Noble MD as an OB-GYN GP, Women's Health Nexus event). This is where PhyCap can build category ownership fastest.

## Gaps in This Section

1. **Physician alternative investment participation rate is estimated, not sourced.** The 10-15% figure for physicians investing in alternatives is based on industry patterns, not physician-specific survey data. AMA or physician financial planning surveys may have more precise data.
2. **No competitor market share analysis.** How much of the SAM is Scrub Capital, Doctors Innovate Fund, or other physician-investor platforms currently capturing? Without this, the OAM estimate is based on total market potential rather than available market share.
3. **FemTech market definition varies widely.** The $2-3B estimate depends on what is included (fertility tech, menopause, maternal health, sexual health, general women's health). PhyCap should define its women's health investment scope more precisely to size this accurately.
4. **Geographic concentration effects unknown.** If PhyCap's GP networks are concentrated in certain metropolitan areas, the effective OAM may be smaller than the national estimate suggests for Fund I, expanding with Fund II.
5. **No data on physician VC fund conversion rates.** What percentage of physicians who attend an event or engage with content eventually become LPs? Industry benchmarks from Scrub Capital or other physician-investor platforms would sharpen the OAM estimate.
6. **SaMD market sizing includes categories PhyCap excludes.** The $50B+ SaMD projection includes pharmaceutical-adjacent and life sciences SaMD, which PhyCap explicitly excludes. A filtered SaMD market size would be more accurate.
