# Audience Segmentation

## Primary Segments

### 1. Physician Limited Partners (LPs)

**Role:** Accredited investor physicians who commit capital to PhyCap Fund I. They are not passive check-writers; they bring clinical expertise, deal-flow referrals, and credibility to portfolio companies.

**Career Stage:** Mid-career to late-career. Typically 10-25+ years in practice. Many hold leadership roles (department chiefs, practice owners, medical directors). Several hold advanced degrees beyond MD (MHCDS, MBA, PhD), signaling investment in systems-level thinking.

**What They Want:**
- A mechanism to influence the direction of healthcare innovation, not just benefit from it financially
- Community with other physicians who think beyond the exam room
- Proof that clinical experience is a legitimate investment lens, not a liability
- Access to deal flow they cannot reach through traditional wealth management
- Intellectual engagement that feels like the best parts of academic medicine without the bureaucracy

**Frustrations:**
- Structural exclusion from the capital decisions that shape the tools they use daily
- Watching non-clinical investors fund solutions that create more documentation burden, not less
- Wealth management products that treat physician capital as interchangeable with any other accredited investor dollar
- The sense that the healthcare system is being redesigned without physician input ("healthcare system is unsustainable" per Brian Russell MD)
- Feeling like a passive consumer of innovation rather than a participant

**Language They Use (drawn from LP testimonials):**
- "A seat at the table" (Emil Engels MD) - signals desire for inclusion in decision-making, not just returns
- "Play an active role in shaping the future of medicine" (Maxwell Ginsberg MD) - emphasizes agency and professional identity
- "Collaborating with colleagues who bring sharp insights" (James Newman MD) - values peer quality, intellectual rigor
- "Healthcare system is unsustainable" (Brian Russell MD) - systemic critique, not complaint; this is a physician who sees the structural problem

**How They Find PhyCap:**
- GP personal networks (Paul Slosar, Robin Noble, Vipul Kella, and other GPs each bring their own clinical communities)
- LinkedIn content from GP personal profiles
- Women's Health Nexus event and similar curated gatherings
- Peer referral from existing LPs (the most reliable channel)
- Medical society conferences and specialty-specific events
- MHCDS and MBA alumni networks (Dartmouth Tuck, other physician-executive programs)

**Why They Join:**
- The GP team is composed of practicing physicians, not finance professionals who hired a medical advisor
- 168+ years of combined clinical experience across 13+ specialties signals legitimacy
- The thesis is narrow and clinically grounded (workflow optimization, SaMD, women's health), which resonates with physicians tired of "spray and pray" digital health funds
- Community model (Innovation Circle) offers ongoing intellectual engagement, not just a capital call
- Events like Women's Health Nexus at Congressional Country Club signal seriousness and peer quality

**Why They Do Not Join:**
- Fund I at $10M is small; some physicians with significant capital prefer larger, more established funds
- Unfamiliarity with VC mechanics creates hesitation (minimum check size, J-curve, illiquidity)
- LEARN and INVEST pages on the website are not publicly accessible, which stalls the education-to-commitment pipeline
- Physicians in academic settings may face conflict-of-interest disclosure requirements that slow the process
- Spouse/partner involvement in financial decisions means the value proposition must translate beyond clinical language

### 2. Healthcare Founders

**Role:** Founders and CEOs of early-stage healthcare technology companies (Seed to Series A) building within PhyCap's thesis areas: clinical care delivery workflow optimization, Software as a Medical Device (FDA Class I/II), and women's health/health inequity solutions.

**Company Stage:** Pre-revenue to early revenue. Typically post-MVP, seeking $150K-$500K checks as part of a larger Seed or Series A round. May have pilot data from 1-3 health systems. Often navigating FDA regulatory pathways for SaMD.

**What They Want:**
- Capital from investors who understand clinical workflows deeply enough to evaluate product-market fit without needing a 45-minute explainer
- Physician champions who can open doors to health system pilots, not just write checks
- Credibility signal: "Our investors include 10 practicing physicians across 13 specialties" is a powerful statement to health system procurement committees
- Guidance on FDA SaMD classification strategy from investors who understand clinical risk
- Warm introductions to the Innovation Circle physician network for clinical validation and early adoption

**Frustrations:**
- General VC partners who ask surface-level clinical questions, then defer to a single "healthcare advisor" who may not know the relevant specialty
- Investors who do not understand the difference between FDA Class I and Class II, or why that distinction matters for go-to-market timeline
- The fundraising process in healthcare, where clinical evidence requirements create longer sales cycles that generalist VCs interpret as "slow traction"
- Getting pattern-matched to failed digital health companies when their product is a fundamentally different clinical tool
- Women's health founders specifically face additional skepticism about market size and clinical significance

**Language They Use:**
- "Clinical validation" rather than "product-market fit" (they think in evidence, not purely in conversion metrics)
- "Workflow integration" rather than "user adoption" (they understand that clinician tools must fit into existing EHR and care delivery patterns)
- "Regulatory pathway" as a standard part of their roadmap, not an afterthought
- "Physician champion" to describe the clinical advocate who will drive adoption within a health system

**How They Find PhyCap:**
- Pitch page on website (currently not publicly accessible, creating friction)
- LinkedIn content, particularly GP posts about specific clinical domains
- Women's Health Nexus event and similar curated gatherings
- Referrals from other healthcare VCs who co-invest (FortySix Venture Capital network)
- Healthcare startup accelerators and incubators
- FDA/regulatory community networks (for SaMD founders)

**Why They Choose PhyCap:**
- 10 GPs across 13 specialties means there is likely a physician on the team who knows the founder's clinical domain firsthand
- $150K-$500K check size is appropriate for Seed-Series A without demanding excessive ownership
- The physician network (Innovation Circle, LP base) offers potential pilot partners, clinical advisors, and early adopters
- PhyCap's thesis alignment (workflow optimization, SaMD, women's health) signals that the team will not need to be educated on why the problem matters
- Co-management with FortySix Venture Capital adds operational VC infrastructure that pure physician groups lack

**Why They Do Not Choose PhyCap:**
- Fund I size ($10M target) limits follow-on investment capacity; founders may worry about pro-rata in Series B
- PITCH page is not publicly accessible, making the initial outreach process unclear
- Founders may question whether physician investors will move at venture speed or operate on clinical-academic timelines
- No publicly visible portfolio beyond BioReact limits social proof for founders evaluating the fund
- Geographic location in Tulsa may create perception of limited coastal deal flow access (despite GP network spanning multiple regions)

## Secondary Segments

### 3. Innovation Circle Community Members

**Role:** Physicians, healthcare executives, and innovation-adjacent professionals who engage with PhyCap's community programming without necessarily committing LP capital. They attend events, consume content, refer deal flow, and may convert to LPs over time.

**Value to PhyCap:** Pipeline for future LPs. Clinical expertise for portfolio company validation. Event attendance that builds brand presence. Content amplification through their own professional networks.

**What They Want:** Intellectual community. Early access to healthcare innovation trends. Networking with physician-leaders outside their specialty. A low-commitment way to explore the intersection of clinical practice and venture capital.

**Conversion Path:** Content consumption (LinkedIn, Resources page) leads to event attendance (Women's Health Nexus) leads to Innovation Circle membership leads to LP commitment. This is a 6-18 month journey for most.

---

### 4. Strategic Partners

**Role:** Organizations that co-invest, co-host events, provide deal flow, or amplify PhyCap's platform. Includes FortySix Venture Capital (co-management partner), health systems exploring innovation partnerships, medical societies, and physician-executive education programs.

**Value to PhyCap:** Operational infrastructure (FortySix), deal flow curation, event co-hosting, credibility by association, distribution channels for content.

**What They Want:** Access to PhyCap's physician network. Co-investment opportunities in clinically validated deals. Thought leadership association with the "physician-led VC" narrative. Event partnerships that reach their target audiences.

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No quantified conversion data.** How many Innovation Circle members have converted to LPs? What is the average time from first touchpoint to LP commitment? Without this data, the conversion funnel remains theoretical.
2. **Founder segment lacks portfolio evidence.** With only BioReact publicly confirmed, the founder value proposition relies on the GP team's credentials rather than demonstrated portfolio outcomes. As Fund I deploys, this gap narrows.
3. **Geographic distribution unknown.** Are physician LPs concentrated in certain regions or specialties? Does the Tulsa HQ create geographic bias in the LP base? This affects event strategy and content targeting.
4. **Spouse/partner influence unmapped.** For physician LPs making $150K-$500K commitments, the household financial decision-maker may not be the physician. No messaging or content currently addresses this audience.
5. **Secondary segment definitions are thin.** Innovation Circle membership criteria, benefits, and engagement metrics need documentation to make this a real segment rather than an aspirational category.
6. **No negative persona defined.** Who is explicitly not a fit? Physicians seeking passive index-fund-style exposure? Founders building in pharma or life sciences? Defining the "not for us" boundary sharpens the segments.
