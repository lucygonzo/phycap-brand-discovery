# Differentiators

## Purpose

These are five things PhyCap can say that no competitor in the physician-led healthcare VC space can also say. Each is backed by specific, verifiable evidence. These are not aspirations. They describe what is actually true right now.

---

## Differentiator 1: Every GP is a Physician, and the Specialties are Named

**The claim:** PhyCap's 10 General Partners contribute 168+ years of combined clinical experience across 13+ medical specialties. Every investment decision-maker is a physician. Not a nurse. Not a pharmacist. Not a hospital administrator. A physician with a named specialty.

**Why no competitor can say this:**
- Scrub Capital's 1,000+ member community spans 40+ specialties but includes nurses, PTs, OTs, and pharmacists. They have 4 GPs to PhyCap's 10. Their clinical insight is real but distributed across credential types and dilutes further with each non-physician member added. When Scrub says "clinician-led," the word "clinician" does significant work. Their Fund 1 closed oversubscribed and they have 13 portfolio companies (including unicorn Midi Health), which validates market demand for clinician-involved investing. The distinction PhyCap must own: physician-led specialist diligence is structurally different from broad clinician community validation.
- Doctors Innovate Fund has physicians as passive LPs through the Alumni Ventures platform, but physicians do not lead diligence or investment decisions at the GP level.
- LRVHealth derives clinical insight from institutional relationships (health system and payer LPs), not from individual physician GPs.
- Rock Health, SV Health Investors, and Catalyst Health Ventures are not physician-led funds.

**Evidence:** GP roster with named individuals, their specialties, and their clinical experience documented in fund materials and team bios.

**Why it matters for founders:** A SaMD founder pitching a cardiology workflow tool talks to a cardiologist, not a committee or a community vote. The feedback is specific, credentialed, and immediately actionable.

**Why it matters for LPs:** The diligence is performed by people who have actually done the clinical work the product claims to improve. LP risk is reduced because the clinical evaluation is expert-level, not generalist.

## Differentiator 2: Three Thesis Areas with Explicit Exclusions

**The claim:** PhyCap invests in three defined areas (Clinical Care Delivery Workflow Optimization, SaMD FDA Class I/II, Women's Health/Health Inequity) and explicitly excludes life sciences and pharmaceuticals.

**Why no competitor can say this:**
- Scrub Capital does not publish exclusions at this level of specificity. Their broader clinician community (1,000+), 13-company portfolio spanning menopause care, spine, fertility, pediatrics, and healthcare worker safety, and check size range ($100K-$300K) all suggest a wider aperture.
- Doctors Innovate Fund operates on a platform model where thesis specificity is constrained by the need to offer broad healthcare exposure to passive physician investors.
- LRVHealth's "Inside Healthcare" positioning is defined by institutional relationships and a Series A/B focus (later stage), not by clinical category focus. Their 25-year, 68-company, 3-unicorn track record is impressive but thesis-agnostic.
- Rock Health Capital ($50M AUM, ~$1.5M sweet spot) and broader healthcare VCs (SV Health, Catalyst) have wider mandates.

**Evidence:** Fund documentation, marketing materials, and the positioning statement all define these three areas and the exclusions.

**Why it matters for founders:** Founders in thesis-aligned areas know immediately that PhyCap understands their category. Founders outside these areas know immediately not to pitch, which saves everyone time. Thesis specificity signals that PhyCap has a point of view, not just a checkbook.

**Why it matters for LPs:** Specificity accelerates pattern recognition. A fund that sees 100 SaMD deals develops better judgment than a fund that sees 20 SaMD deals and 80 deals across other categories. LPs benefit from concentrated expertise.

## Differentiator 3: A Proprietary Event Franchise in Women's Health

**The claim:** PhyCap created and owns the Women's Health Nexus, a proprietary event that drew 200+ attendees to Congressional Country Club in November 2025. No other physician-led VC fund has a comparable owned event in Women's Health.

**Why no competitor can say this:**
- Scrub Capital co-hosted "Healthcare, Unscripted" (March 2026, NYC, 100 attendees) with Primary Ventures, but this is an unconference format, not a category-specific proprietary event. It is co-branded, smaller, and not tied to a thesis area the way Women's Health Nexus is tied to PhyCap's Women's Health focus.
- Doctors Innovate Fund relies on Alumni Ventures' platform events, which are not category-specific or fund-specific.
- Healthy Ventures is women-led but does not own a Women's Health event platform.
- Rock Health hosts events and summits, but these are industry-wide digital health events, not Women's Health-specific.

**Evidence:** Women's Health Nexus, November 2025, Congressional Country Club, 200+ attendees. These are verifiable facts.

**Why it matters for founders:** Women's Health founders know that PhyCap is building the ecosystem around the category, not just deploying capital. An investor who convenes 200+ people in your market is an investor who can make introductions, build awareness, and accelerate adoption.

**Why it matters for LPs:** The event demonstrates operational capability, category commitment, and community-building skill. It also functions as a deal flow and LP recruitment channel, which means the investment in the event generates returns across multiple dimensions.

## Differentiator 4: Check Size Range that Enables Lead Positioning at Seed

**The claim:** PhyCap writes checks from $150K to $500K at Seed through Series A. The upper end of this range can lead a seed round, giving PhyCap board or observer seats and meaningful influence on company direction.

**Why no competitor can say this:**
- Scrub Capital writes $100K-$300K checks. Their upper bound is PhyCap's near-lower bound. At $300K, leading a seed round is possible but limiting. At $500K, it is routine. Scrub's oversubscribed Fund 1 (beyond $10M target) may allow larger checks in a future fund, but the current range constrains their lead positioning.
- Doctors Innovate Fund invests through a platform model where individual fund check sizes are constrained by platform economics.
- Rock Health Capital writes $100K-$5M (sweet spot ~$1.5M) and could overlap at seed, but they have only 4 portfolio companies and no clinical depth positioning. Larger healthcare VCs (SV Health, LRVHealth at Series A/B) write bigger checks at later stages and do not compete for seed-stage lead positions in the same categories.

**Evidence:** Fund documentation specifying $150K-$500K check size range for Seed to Series A investments.

**Why it matters for founders:** A lead investor sets terms, coordinates the round, and serves as the primary board-level partner. Founders building SaMD or clinical workflow tools at seed stage want a lead investor with clinical depth, and PhyCap can be that lead. Scrub Capital, at $100K-$300K, is more often a follow-on participant.

**Why it matters for LPs:** Leading rounds at seed generates better ownership economics (lower valuations, meaningful equity stakes) than following in later rounds. The check size range positions PhyCap for ownership levels that drive fund returns.

## Differentiator 5: Tulsa HQ with National Thesis Reach

**The claim:** PhyCap is headquartered in Tulsa, Oklahoma, making it one of the only physician-led healthcare VC funds based outside the coastal VC corridors of Boston, San Francisco, and New York.

**Why no competitor can say this:**
- Scrub Capital operates from traditional VC geographies.
- Doctors Innovate Fund inherits Alumni Ventures' Boston-area base.
- LRVHealth is Boston-based.
- Rock Health is San Francisco-based.
- SV Health Investors is global but anchored in established VC markets.

**Evidence:** Fund registration and operational headquarters in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

**Why it matters for founders:** Midwest and Southern healthcare systems are massive markets that coastal VCs often underserve. A Tulsa-based fund with national thesis reach sees deal flow from regions where physician adoption patterns, health system structures, and clinical workflows differ meaningfully from coastal academic medical centers.

**Why it matters for LPs:** Tulsa headquarters creates a cost structure advantage (lower operating costs than Boston or San Francisco) and access to physician networks in regions with high clinical volume and underexplored innovation ecosystems. It also signals authenticity: PhyCap is building from a place of clinical practice, not from a place of VC convention.

## Differentiator Durability Assessment

| Differentiator | Durability | Risk of Competitor Replication |
|---------------|------------|-------------------------------|
| Every GP is a physician with named specialties | High | Recruiting 10+ physician GPs across 13+ specialties takes years of relationship building. Scrub Capital has 4 GPs; scaling GP count while maintaining physician-only status is structurally difficult for a broad-clinician model. |
| Three thesis areas with explicit exclusions | Medium | Thesis can be copied, but pattern recognition from deployment cannot |
| Women's Health Nexus event franchise | High | First-mover advantage in category-specific events is durable. Scrub Capital's "Healthcare, Unscripted" (100 attendees, co-branded) is general and smaller than WHN (200+ attendees, proprietary). |
| Check size enabling seed lead position | Medium | Check size is a function of fund size and can be matched by any fund that raises comparable capital |
| Tulsa HQ | High | Geographic identity is inherent and cannot be replicated; it either is or is not your headquarters |

## Gaps in This Section

1. **No founder testimonial or case study validating the clinical diligence advantage.** Differentiator 1 is structurally strong, but a founder saying "the cardiologist GP caught something our other investors missed" would make it concrete.
2. **Check size range may evolve with Fund II.** Differentiator 4 is specific to Fund I economics. Fund II may have a different check size range, which would change the positioning.
3. **Tulsa HQ advantage is asserted but not measured.** Differentiator 5 claims access to Midwest/Southern deal flow, but the actual deal flow data by geography is not documented.
4. **No LP perspective on which differentiator matters most.** These five differentiators are presented without data on which ones drive LP commitment decisions. Some may be compelling in pitches; others may be table stakes.
5. **Specialty coverage across the GP team is referenced (13+) but not listed by name in this document.** Including the specific specialties would strengthen Differentiator 1 with specificity.
